20Mimetic Desire

The psychology of wanting.

René Girard's theory of Mimetic Desire reveals that humans don't know what they want; they imitate the desires of others. In elite B2B sales, this is how you transform a logical software purchase into an irresistible object of status and FOMO.

What is Mimesis?

Standard economic theory assumes buyers act rationally based on an objective assessment of their needs. René Girard's Mimetic Theory states the exact opposite: all human desire is borrowed.

We don't desire an object for its intrinsic qualities. We desire an object because another person—whom we admire, envy, or view as a rival—desires it. This is why Rolex watches and Hermes bags sell for vastly more than the cost of their materials. The buyer is purchasing the status associated with the brand's other owners.

Identifying the Model (The 'Who')

In B2B sales, mimetic desire is triggered by industry peers and rivals. To activate it, you must identify who your prospect views as their "Model."

The Principle of Proximity

A prospect at a mid-market logistics firm in Ohio doesn't care that Google or Amazon uses your software. They aren't trying to be Google. They care deeply that their direct rival across town, or the fastest-growing logistics firm in Chicago, is using it.

Your discovery process must uncover the prospect's models. "Who in your space is doing this really well right now?" Once you know who they admire (or envy), you position your product as the vehicle that those specific models use to achieve dominance.

Engineered FOMO

When selling, you can engineer Mimetic FOMO by shifting the conversation away from features and toward the industry shift.

  • Weak approach (Feature): "Our software processes data 3x faster."
  • Mimetic approach (Desire): "Right now, the top 10% of agencies we work with—like [Model A] and [Model B]—are aggressively automating this exact process. They see it as an arms race. If you aren't automating this by Q3, you simply won't be able to compete on margin with them."

Notice the shift. The buyer is no longer evaluating software; they are evaluating their survival and status within their peer group.

B2B Status Games

B2B buyers are human. They want to be promoted, they want to look smart in front of their board, and they want to be seen as innovators. This is the personal win.

Elite sellers map their product to the buyer's internal status game. When you sell an enterprise platform, you are selling the buyer the opportunity to stand up at the end-of-year all-hands meeting and announce a massive efficiency gain that makes them look like a visionary.

You are not selling software. You are selling the buyer a promotion.
