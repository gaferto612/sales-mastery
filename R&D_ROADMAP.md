# Sales Mastery: research and development roadmap

This roadmap translates the second R&D review into concrete curriculum changes. It is
intended to sit beside `RESEARCH.md` and `EVIDENCE.md`; it does not turn practitioner
frameworks into evidence.

## Findings that should shape the next release

### Adaptive selling and listening are core capabilities

Meta-analyses find that adaptive selling is positively related to performance and that it
mediates the value of customer orientation. A separate meta-analytic model finds that
listening supports adaptation, relational outcomes, and sales outcomes. The course covers
adaptation indirectly, but listening deserves an explicit, observable skill standard.

**Add:** a short core module or lesson, “Listening, sense-making, and conversational
adaptation,” with transcript annotation, reflective listening, clarification, and a
scored role-play. Do not teach a talk-ratio target as a goal; use conversation balance as
diagnostic evidence only.

Sources: Franke & Park (2006), Itani, Goad & Jaramillo (2019).

### Forecasting needs calibration, not confidence

Forecasting research treats judgment as useful but bias-prone. The current forecasting
module should require probability estimates, a reason code, disconfirming evidence, and a
post-period calibration review. A manager should compare forecast confidence with actual
outcomes instead of rewarding optimism.

**Add:** a forecast lab using historical deal scenarios. Learners submit 20/40/60/80%
probabilities, then calculate calibration error and Brier score after outcomes are
revealed. Keep stage labels as process evidence, not fixed probabilities.

Source: Lawrence, Goodwin, O'Connor & Önkal (2006), *International Journal of
Forecasting*.

### Learning design should use spacing and retrieval

The site currently rewards completion, but completion is not durable learning. Reviews and
meta-analyses support spaced retrieval over massed rereading and show that transfer is
strongest when learners retrieve, explain, and apply knowledge in changed contexts.

**Add:** a 1–3–7–21 day review queue; interleave questions from earlier levels; and attach
one transfer prompt to every level (“use this model in a different industry or motion”).
Track recall and artifact quality separately from page completion.

Sources: Carpenter, Pan & Butler (2022); Latimier, Peyre & Ramus (2021); Kauffeld &
Lehmann-Willenbrock (2010).

### Customer success belongs in the value loop

Recent B2B research describes customer success as proactive work to increase realized
value and retention, not merely support after the sale. Module 11 should therefore connect
the original value hypothesis to adoption evidence, time-to-value, risk signals, renewal,
and expansion.

**Add:** a post-sale value review artifact with baseline, adoption behavior, realized
outcome, unresolved risk, owner, and next value milestone. Do not treat a generic “health
score” as evidence without defining its inputs and validation.

Source: Hochstein et al. (2023), *International Journal of Research in Marketing*.

### Ethics should include organizational incentives

The course handles individual manipulation well. Research also indicates that firm-level
relationships and incentive systems can shape unethical upselling and selling behavior.

**Add:** a manager exercise that audits quota, compensation, contest, and escalation
design. Ask learners to identify which pressure could make a reasonable seller overclaim,
hide risk, or sell an unsuitable product, then redesign the control.

## Curriculum architecture changes

1. Mark Modules 20–22 and 27–30 as optional interpretive lenses and require completion of
   the core evidence modules first.
2. Add “different from” boxes to discovery/qualification, negotiation/objections,
   retention/expansion, and forecasting/RevOps clusters.
3. Add one transfer case per level outside the Brayford storyline.
4. Replace one quiz in every level with an assessed artifact and feedback rubric.
5. Add a diagnostic entry assessment that routes learners to a minimum viable path.
6. Add a source ID beside every exact number; if a number has no boundary condition, remove
   it.

## What not to add

- More named sales frameworks without evidence or a clear use case.
- Universal talk/listen, touch-count, conversion, or personality rules.
- Neuroscience or “buyer type” diagnoses inferred from observable behavior.
- Extra modules that duplicate discovery, negotiation, or account planning without a new
  decision, artifact, or failure mechanism.
