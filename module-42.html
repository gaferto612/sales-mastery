42Global & Public Sector

Adapt to institutions without stereotyping people.

Navigate culture, regulation, public procurement, localization, and multi-country execution with evidence, humility, and compliance.

45 min lesson + practiceEvidence-led professional curriculumLevel 5
In this module
  • 1. Research institutional context
  • 2. Use culture as a question
  • 3. Localize the whole offer
  • 4. Understand public procurement
  • 5. Sell into regulated risk
  • 6. Choose local execution carefully
  • 7. Artifact: market-entry and bid brief

1. Research institutional context

Map laws, procurement rules, budget cycles, decision rights, data requirements, standards, labor environment, payment practices, language, and local alternatives. National averages do not describe an individual buyer or organization.

2. Use culture as a question

Prepare for differences in directness, hierarchy, time, relationships, risk, and contracting, but verify preferences. Use cultural frameworks to generate questions. Never infer competence, honesty, or authority from nationality.

3. Localize the whole offer

Adapt evidence, currency, tax, payment, implementation, support hours, accessibility, data location, integrations, legal terms, pricing, and examples—not only language. Use qualified local experts for law and regulation.

4. Understand public procurement

Public buyers may require transparent competition, formal evaluation, registrations, mandatory clauses, past performance, and audit-safe documentation. Read the solicitation literally, follow communication rules, and distinguish responsiveness, technical merit, and price.

5. Sell into regulated risk

Map control owners and evidence early. Build traceability from requirement to response to proof. Never promise compliance as a vague product property; it depends on use, configuration, jurisdiction, and governance.

6. Choose local execution carefully

Assess agents, distributors, and subcontractors for capability, conflicts, ownership, sanctions, anti-bribery risk, data handling, and reputation. Define oversight. “That is how business is done here” is not a compliance defense.

7. Artifact: market-entry and bid brief

Create an institutional map, cultural-question plan, localization checklist, procurement calendar, compliance matrix, partner due diligence, bid/no-bid decision, and risk-adjusted entry plan.
