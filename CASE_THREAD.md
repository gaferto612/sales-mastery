# The Brayford Thread — one deal, forty-three modules

This is the continuity reference for the case thread that runs through every module. Each
module contains an **"Applied to the Brayford deal"** box showing how that module's specific
tool acts on the state of this one opportunity at that point in its life.

The purpose is not decoration. A learner who meets discovery, business cases, procurement,
and renewal as unrelated exercises never sees the thing that actually makes complex selling
hard: **that a decision made in week 4 constrains what is available in week 24.** The thread
exists so that constraint is visible.

Every module box must be consistent with this document. If a module needs a fact that is not
here, add it here first.

---

## 1. The seller

**Cadence** — workforce scheduling and absence-cover software.

| Fact | Value |
|------|-------|
| Founded | 2022, two founders |
| Stage at the time of this deal | ~£8.1m ARR, 46 staff |
| Motion | Self-serve tier for single-site operators; assisted and enterprise motions above 5 sites |
| Core mechanism | Surfaces which internal bank staff are genuinely available for a specific shift and skill mix, at the moment a gap appears |
| ICP (validated on retained cohorts) | Multi-site care and healthcare operators, 5–25 sites, with a named workforce-systems owner and an API-accessible rostering system |
| Anti-ICP | Single-site; workforce function outsourced; no API on the source system; incumbent framework with 18+ months remaining |
| Known weakness | Reporting. The audit-trail export needs a manual reconciliation step, roughly two hours a month for one person. Roadmap says Q3; it has slipped once already |
| Genuine competitor advantage | Stafford's reporting and approval-chain modelling are better than Cadence's, and Cadence says so |

**Seller on the deal:** Alex Rowe, account executive, 3 years at Cadence.

## 2. The buyer

**Brayford Care Group** — residential and nursing care.

| Fact | Value |
|------|-------|
| Sites | 14 original homes across the North of England, plus 4 acquired from **Marlow Homes** nine months ago = **18** |
| Staff | ~1,900, of whom ~1,150 are shift-based care staff |
| Systems | Original 14 sites on **Rotaflow** (incumbent, renewal due September). Marlow's 4 sites on a separate legacy system with no API |
| Regulator | CQC (Care Quality Commission) |
| Structure | Group operations centralised; regional managers own site clusters |

### The problem, stated as a mechanism

> Same-day absence cover costs Brayford roughly **£2.4m a year in agency spend**, of which
> about **40% is booked same-day at premium rates**, *because* the bank-staff availability
> list is a spreadsheet updated weekly — so at 06:40 a home manager facing an uncovered
> shift phones the agency rather than spending twenty minutes ringing round bank staff who
> may already have been booked elsewhere.

Note the shape: this is a *because* statement (Module 33), and the cost is Brayford's own
figure, not Cadence's estimate.

### The trigger

Two events, nine weeks apart:

1. A **CQC inspection report** (published, public) citing staffing-record inconsistencies at
   two of the acquired Marlow sites.
2. Agency spend **up 34%** post-acquisition, flagged in the group's own half-year board pack.

## 5. The numbers

Every figure a module may cite. Marked with its evidence class (see `RESEARCH.md`).

| Figure | Value | Class | Source in the story |
|--------|-------|-------|---------------------|
| Total agency spend | £2.4m/yr across 18 sites | Fact | Brayford's own finance report |
| Same-day share of agency spend | ~40% | Estimate | Priya's estimate, week 4; never measured precisely |
| Uncovered shifts requiring same-day cover | ~26/week group-wide | Fact | Rotaflow export |
| Premium over standard bank rate | 1.8× | Fact | Brayford's agency framework rates |
| Bank staff on the books | 340 | Fact | HR system |
| Bank staff actually reachable at 06:40 | **Unknown** | — | *This is the load-bearing unknown of the whole deal* |
| Comparable result, 3 similar operators | Same-day agency bookings fell **19–31%** in 6 months | Class V — Cadence's own deployments | The 31% case had an unusually large bank; the low end is the honest expectation |
| The failed comparable | One operator: **no improvement at all** | Class V | Their bank-staff records were on three systems and never became usable |
| Original proposal | £480k / 3 years / 18 sites | — | Week 12 |
| Signed | **£312k / 3 years / 4 sites** | — | Week 27 |
| Expansion (month 18) | £510k, all 18 sites | — | — |
| Elaine's payback threshold | 30 months | Fact | Stated at the capital committee |
| Modelled payback at 4 sites | 22 months | Estimate | Sensitive to the adoption assumption |

### The assumption ledger

The business case rests on three assumptions. The course returns to these repeatedly:

1. **Observed** — 26 uncovered shifts a week needing same-day cover. From Rotaflow's own export.
2. **Inferred** — that a 19–31% reduction transfers to Brayford. From three comparable
   deployments, none of which had a post-acquisition dual-system estate.
3. **Guessed** — that recovered agency spend is *cash*, not just avoided premium. It depends
   on whether Brayford would otherwise have hired substantively. **This became a question for
   Elaine rather than a number in a deck**, and that decision is why the case survived the
   capital committee.

## 6. The five decision points

These are the moments the thread exists to teach. Each is a place where an earlier choice
constrained a later option.

| # | Moment | The choice | What it cost or bought later |
|---|--------|-----------|------------------------------|
| **1** | Week 4 — discovery | Asked *"is that consistent, or does it move?"* rather than *"what's your biggest challenge?"* | Found the 06:40 mechanism. Without it the proposal would have been a generic rostering pitch, and Stafford wins on reporting |
| **2** | Week 6 — demo | Disclosed the reporting weakness unprompted | Cost the reporting criterion at week 9. Bought the credibility that survived weeks 14–20 |
| **3** | Week 16 — the stall | Asked Tom whether he actually had a route to Elaine, instead of sending more evidence | Unblocked a deal that was six weeks stalled on a misdiagnosed champion. Sending more ROI would have made it worse |
| **4** | Week 18 — governance | Engaged Dev Osei 22 weeks before signature | The deal survives. Engaged at week 40 it would have died at 90% confidence |
| **5** | Week 27 — scope | Signed 4 sites instead of pushing for 18 | Lower bookings, higher realized value. The 18-site version had no IG clearance for the Marlow estate and would have failed in implementation |

## 7. What went wrong

The thread is not a success story with the friction removed. Three things genuinely failed,
and modules must not pretend otherwise:

- **Tom was misread as a champion for twelve weeks.** He was supportive, responsive, and
  entirely unable to move the deal. Alex mistook enthusiasm for advocacy — the single most
  common error in the whole course.
- **Graham Hale was never mapped.** His opposition was invisible until week 23 and was
  guessed wrong when it did surface: the assumption was headcount protection, but the actual
  driver was a public commitment he had made to the Marlow board about system continuity.
- **The reporting gap cost real ground.** Disclosing it was correct and it still lost points
  at week 9. Honesty is not free; the course claims only that it is cheaper than the
  alternative.
