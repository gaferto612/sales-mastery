23Qualification

A filled-in framework is not evidence.

MEDDPICC is an inspection instrument that tells you what you do not know. It fails the moment a seller fills the letters with assertions instead of buyer evidence — which is what most teams do. This module rebuilds it as an evidence grid and pairs it with a mutual action plan that actually predicts.

1. What qualification frameworks are for

MEDDIC and its extensions are class F — structures for organizing work. They are not a selling method and they do not tell you how to find anything out. Their entire function is to make the shape of your ignorance visible, so that a manager can ask about it and a forecast can be built on something.

They fail in a specific and predictable way. A seller is asked to complete the fields, so they complete the fields — with what they believe, what the champion told them, or what sounds plausible. The CRM now shows a fully qualified deal. Nothing has been learned, and the framework has actively made things worse by converting uncertainty into the appearance of knowledge.

The fix is a single discipline applied everywhere: every entry must cite buyer evidence, not seller belief. Not "the CFO is the economic buyer" but "the CFO is the economic buyer — confirmed by the champion on 14 March, and separately by the procurement lead who named the £250k approval threshold." Not "they need this by Q3" but "the FCA remediation deadline is 30 September, from their published board minutes."

Applied that way, the framework does something valuable and slightly uncomfortable: it usually reveals that deals you were confident about are largely undocumented, and that the missing entries cluster in the same two or three places across your whole pipeline. That clustering is a coaching finding worth more than any individual deal.

2. Observable learning objectives

By completing this module you will be able to demonstrate the following:

Populate every MEDDPICC element with cited buyer evidence and a date, distinguishing confirmed from assumed.

Apply a behavioral champion test rather than an enthusiasm judgment.

Identify which elements are missing across your pipeline and what that pattern indicates.

Build a mutual action plan with buyer-owned tasks and buyer-proposed dates.

Use the grid to make a defensible go / restructure / disqualify decision.

The three-state rule

Score every element as confirmed (buyer evidence with a source and date), assumed (plausible, uncited), or unknown. Most teams collapse "assumed" into "confirmed," which is precisely how a forecast becomes fiction. Keeping the middle state honest is the single change that makes the whole framework work — and it makes deal reviews faster, because the conversation goes straight to the assumed and unknown entries.

Two elements deserve special weight because they kill deals late. Paper process is routinely ignored until after the decision, at which point six weeks appear from nowhere. And competition including do-nothing is usually recorded as a vendor list, when the actual leading competitor in most markets is the status quo — which no rival will help you defeat.

4. Testing the champion

The champion field is where optimism concentrates, because enthusiasm is pleasant and easy to mistake for advocacy. The test must be behavioral.

Table 2: The three champion behaviors

BehaviorEvidence it happenedWhat its absence means
1. Grants access upwardThey arranged a meeting with someone senior to them, or with a veto holder.Either they lack standing, or they are not convinced. Ask which — the answer is usually the first, and they are embarrassed to say so.
2. Tells you inconvenient truthsThey disclosed a competitor's advantage, a budget risk, or internal opposition, unprompted.You are being managed rather than partnered with. You will learn about the obstacle after it has decided the outcome.
3. Acts when you are not thereThey can describe something specific they did between meetings, with a date.The deal only moves when you push it, which means it is your project, not theirs — and it will stop when your attention does.

Two of three is a working champion. One or fewer is a coach: valuable for information, unreliable for advocacy, and the single most common cause of a confident forecast on a dead deal. Relabel them in the CRM — the relabelling is the point, because it changes what you do next.

What to do with a coach

Do not abandon them and do not press harder. Ask directly and without blame: "I've been working as though you'd take this to the CFO yourself. Is that fair, or am I putting you in an awkward position?" This gives them a face-saving route to tell you the truth, which is usually that they lack the standing. Then do the work for them: build the two-page artifact they can forward without personal risk (Module 06), and find a second entry point in parallel.

5. Mutual action plans that predict

A mutual action plan is a shared, dated list of every remaining step to a decision, with an owner on each. Its value is not administrative — it is predictive, because buyer-owned tasks are small commitments and their completion or slippage is the earliest reliable signal about the deal.

Table 3: What separates a working MAP from a project schedule

PropertyWorking MAPProject schedule pretending to be a MAP
AuthorshipCo-written in the meeting, on a shared screen.Written by the seller and emailed as a PDF.
Buyer-owned tasksAt least three, with named individuals.Zero or one, usually "review and provide feedback."
DatesProposed by the buyer, working backwards from their decision date.Proposed by the seller, working backwards from quarter end.
Backward passStarts from the decision date and works back through paper process, security, and legal — which usually reveals the real start date is already past.Starts today and works forward, so the deadline is whatever the seller needs.
ReviewOpened at the top of every call.Never referenced again.
EditsThe buyer has changed it at least once.Untouched since creation.
Predictive valueHigh — a missed buyer task is an early warning weeks before the forecast would show it.Zero.

The backward pass deserves emphasis because it is the most useful and least performed step. Start from the buyer's required go-live or decision date and work backwards through every dependency: legal review, security questionnaire, procurement's three-quote rule, board paper deadlines, holiday periods. In a large proportion of enterprise deals this exercise shows that the buyer's stated timeline was never achievable — and discovering that in March, jointly, is a completely different conversation from discovering it in August.

Outcome: Deal A closed on schedule. Deal B was disqualified six weeks later — the coach had no route to the COO, the budget had been redirected to a compliance programme in January, and nobody had asked. The capacity returned went into two deals that closed that quarter. Both deals had looked identical in the CRM, which is exactly the failure mode the three-state rule exists to catch.

The Brayford thread · Week 16 · the grid that was mostly assumptions

Seven of eight complete, one of eight confirmed

At week 16 the Brayford CRM record showed seven of eight MEDDPICC elements populated and the deal at 70%. Scored honestly with the three-state rule, it looked different.

Confirmed: Metrics (Priya's own agency figures, sourced and dated). Identify pain (the CQC report, published). Assumed: Economic buyer — "Elaine signs it off", said once by Tom in week 6, never verified. Decision criteria — Tom's requirements document, mistaken for the group's criteria. Champion — Tom, who failed all three behavioural tests. Competition — "we're ahead of Stafford", based on tone. Unknown: Decision process, and paper process entirely.

One confirmed, five assumed, two unknown. The forecast moved from 70% to 25% on that scoring alone, before anything about the deal changed.

The backward pass from the week-25 committee date produced the finding that mattered most: papers were due ten days before, IG sign-off had to precede papers, and IG had not started. The date by which the security pack needed to be sent had already passed. It went out that afternoon.

7. Weak versus improved qualification conversation

Composite Case StudyScenario: The seller needs the decision process and the paper process, and does not want the conversation to feel like an audit.

Weak execution (interrogation with the framework showing)

Seller: "A few qualification questions so I can update our system. Who's the economic buyer? What's your decision criteria? What's the decision process? What's the timeline? And is there a formal procurement process?"
Buyer: "Er — the usual, I suppose. It'd go to the leadership team. Timeline, ideally this quarter."
Seller: "Great, thanks." [Fills in six fields with that answer.]

Why it fails: The questions serve the seller's CRM and the buyer can hear it — "so I can update our system" says so explicitly. Rapid-fire abstract questions produce abstract answers, which is what "the usual, I suppose" is. Nothing in the reply is evidence, yet six fields now read as complete. And the buyer has learned that this seller's questions are administrative, which reduces what they volunteer for the rest of the cycle.

Improved execution (past-event questions, framed as risk reduction for them)

Seller: "Can I ask about something that isn't really about us? Most of the delays I see aren't about whether people want the thing — they're about a step nobody mapped. So I'd rather find those now than in August.

Think about the last time you bought something of a similar size — what actually happened? Not what's supposed to happen, but the real sequence."
Buyer: "The analytics platform, last year. That was… painful, actually. It went to the leadership team, then legal took about three weeks, then procurement came back and said we needed two more quotes, which added a month."
Seller: "That's really useful. Two more quotes — is that a threshold thing?"
Buyer: "Anything over £200k, yes."
Seller: "So we'd be in that. Who chases the other quotes, you or procurement?"
Buyer: "Us, unfortunately."
Seller: "Then let's put that on the plan early rather than discovering it after the decision. And on the leadership team — is there a papers deadline before it can be tabled?"
Buyer: "Ten days before. And they don't meet in August."
Seller: "Then let's work backwards from that together, because I think it changes when we need to start the security review."

Why it succeeds: The framing puts the buyer's risk first, honestly. The core question is about a past event rather than a process, which produces the real sequence rather than the official one — this single substitution is the most reliable qualification technique in the module. Each follow-up comes from what they actually said. And the August blackout, which would have destroyed the timeline, surfaced in a conversation that never felt like an audit.

Where this fails

Where even experienced sellers get this wrong

Experienced teams adopt the three-state discipline and then apply it to fields rather than to freshness. An element gets marked confirmed in week 6 with a proper source and date, and stays green for the rest of the cycle.

But confirmations expire. "Elaine is the economic buyer, confirmed by the finance business partner on 3 March" is a solid entry in March and an assumption by September, because the approval threshold may have changed, the capital committee may have been reconstituted, or Elaine may have moved. At Brayford the decision criteria were confirmed in week 9 and were already stale by week 23, when Graham Hale's objection reopened the question of what the group was actually optimising.

The seller reads a green field and stops asking. The green is a claim about a conversation that happened five months ago.

Give each confirmation an expiry proportionate to the element: economic buyer and decision process, six weeks; paper process, once per quarter; criteria, whenever a new stakeholder enters. An expired confirmation reverts to assumed automatically, and the reversion is the prompt to go and look.

The mechanism: Qualification records the state of knowledge at a moment and displays it as current. Because confirmation is effortful and re-confirmation feels redundant, entries age silently — and the elements that change most during a long cycle are precisely the ones whose staleness is least visible, since nothing in the record distinguishes a fact from a memory.

8. Practice: the MEDDPICC evidence grid

Exercise goal: produce an honest evidence grid for a live deal, apply the champion test behaviorally, build a real mutual action plan with a backward pass, and reach a defensible decision.

Applies to your real situation
  1. Take a deal you currently forecast at 60% or above. Complete all eight elements with the source and date of the evidence for each.
  2. Score each as confirmed / assumed / unknown. Resist upgrading anything without a citation.
  3. Apply the three-part champion test. Record which behaviors have actually been observed, with examples. Relabel to "coach" if fewer than two.
  4. Write the paper process in full — legal, security, procurement thresholds, data agreements — with an elapsed-time estimate drawn from their last comparable purchase, not from your hope.
  5. Write the competition entry including do-nothing, and state what would make do-nothing win.
  6. Build the mutual action plan with a backward pass from the buyer's decision date. Include at least three buyer-owned tasks with named individuals.
  7. Identify the earliest date on the backward pass that has already passed, if any. That is your timeline problem, and it should be raised jointly.
  8. Make the call: go, restructure, or disqualify — and write the one sentence justifying it.
  9. Repeat across your top five deals and look for which element is habitually missing. That pattern is the coaching finding.

Evaluation rubric: Every element cited with source and date (25%); honest three-state scoring with assumed entries left as assumed (20%); champion tested behaviorally with examples (15%); paper process estimated from the buyer's last comparable purchase (15%); MAP with a backward pass and three or more buyer-owned tasks (15%); explicit go/restructure/disqualify call with a one-sentence justification (10%).

Beginner variation: Do steps 1–3 for one deal. Most sellers find that a deal they forecast confidently has two or three elements that are assumed rather than confirmed, and the exercise takes twenty minutes.

Advanced variation: Run the grid across the whole team's top deals and aggregate which element is most often assumed or unknown. It is usually paper process or economic buyer, and it is a systemic coaching finding — one that a stage dictionary (Module 39) can enforce far more reliably than exhortation.

9. Applied knowledge check

1. Why can a fully completed MEDDPICC record make a forecast worse?

Because completion is not evidence. A seller asked to fill fields will fill them — with beliefs, with what the champion said, with plausible inferences — and the CRM then displays a qualified deal where none exists, converting uncertainty into the appearance of knowledge. The fix is the three-state rule: mark every element confirmed (with a cited source and date), assumed, or unknown, and never let "assumed" quietly become "confirmed." Deal reviews also get faster, because the conversation goes straight to the assumed and unknown entries.

2. What are the three behaviors that distinguish a champion from a coach?

Granting access upward, telling you inconvenient truths unprompted, and taking specific action when you are not present. Two of three is a working champion; one or fewer is a coach. The distinction matters because a coach is genuinely useful for information and unreliable for advocacy, and mistaking one for the other is the most common cause of a confident forecast on a dead deal. When someone fails the access test, ask directly and without blame — the reason is usually that they lack standing and are embarrassed to say so.

3. Why ask "what happened last time you bought something this size?" rather than "what's your decision process?"

Because past-event questions produce the real sequence and process questions produce the official one. People describe how purchasing is supposed to work when asked abstractly, and they describe what actually happened — legal took three weeks, procurement demanded two more quotes, the board does not meet in August — when asked about a specific event they remember. This single substitution is the most reliable qualification technique in the module, and it does not feel like an audit.

4. What is a backward pass and why does it matter?

Working backwards from the buyer's required decision or go-live date through every dependency — legal review, security sign-off, procurement's quote thresholds, board paper deadlines, holiday blackouts — to find when each step must actually start. It matters because in a large share of enterprise deals it reveals that the stated timeline was never achievable, and often that a step should have started already. Discovering that jointly in March is an entirely different conversation from discovering it alone in August.

5. Two deals both show seven of eight elements complete. What should you look at?

The state of each entry and the champion test. One deal may have seven confirmed entries with sources and dates and a 3/3 champion; the other may have one confirmed, six assumed, and an enthusiastic coach with no upward access. In the CRM they look identical. The evidence grid exists precisely to separate them — and the second deal's correct treatment is not more effort but a direct conversation with the coach, a forwardable artifact, a second entry point, and a hard re-qualification date.

10. Practical conclusion & next action

Qualification frameworks are inspection instruments, and an instrument that reports what you hoped rather than what you know is worse than none. Everything useful about MEDDPICC follows from one discipline: cite the buyer evidence, or mark it assumed.

  • MEDDPICC tells you what you do not know. It does not tell you how to find out — that is discovery.
  • Every entry needs a source and a date. Score confirmed / assumed / unknown and keep the middle state honest.
  • Test the champion behaviorally: upward access, inconvenient truths, independent action. Two of three or relabel.
  • Estimate paper process from the buyer's last comparable purchase, never from your hope.
  • Record do-nothing as a competitor and state what would make it win.
  • A MAP with no buyer-owned tasks and no buyer edits has zero predictive value.
  • Do the backward pass. It routinely shows the timeline was never real.

Immediate action: Take your largest deal and mark every MEDDPICC element confirmed, assumed, or unknown, with a source and date for the confirmed ones. Then do the backward pass from the buyer's stated decision date. Most sellers find at least one dependency that should already have started.
