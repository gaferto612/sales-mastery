06Copywriting & Marketing

Salesmanship in print.

Copywriting is salesmanship in print — John E. Kennedy's 1904 definition, still the cleanest one. Every piece of marketing has the same goal as a salesperson in a conversation: move someone, one decision at a time.

The single most important rule

Write to one person.

Not "audiences." Not "users." Not "marketers aged 25–45." One specific human. Picture them. Name them. Imagine them reading.

Generic marketing speak Our platform empowers teams to streamline workflows.
Written to one person If you've ever lost an hour finding a file someone Slacked you three weeks ago, you'll get why we built this.

The second is also for everyone, but it feels for someone.

The hierarchy of copy

Every piece of writing has parts that matter unequally:

  1. Headline — 80% of the work. If the headline fails, the rest never gets read.
  2. Subhead / hook — confirms the promise, earns the next read
  3. Lead — sells the rest of the copy, not the product
  4. Body — proof, mechanism, benefits, story
  5. Offer — what they get, why now, risk reversal
  6. CTA — what to do next, exactly

Amateurs spend 90% of effort on body. Pros spend 50% on headline and lead alone.

Content marketing pyramid

Instead of paying for attention, you earn it by publishing things people actually want to consume.

1× Pillar piece (long-form video, podcast, blog) per week
↓ atomized into
3–5× Mid-form (threads, LinkedIn posts, carousels)
↓ atomized into
Daily Short-form (tweets, reels, shorts, stories)

One pillar piece becomes 20 atomized posts. This is how solo creators look prolific.

Anatomy of a high-performing email

  • Subject line — 4–8 words, curiosity or specificity, no salesy hype
  • Preview text — extends the subject, adds intrigue
  • Opening line — relevant to them, not "Hope you're well"
  • Single message — one idea, one CTA
  • Plain text often beats HTML — people reply to emails that look like emails

Voice & tone

The single biggest copy differentiator in 2026 isn't tactics — it's voice.

To find your voice

  • Write the way you actually talk
  • Read it out loud — if you wouldn't say it, don't write it
  • Use specific words, not corporate ones
  • Have opinions; bland is invisible
  • Repeat your phrases; consistency builds recognition

Words banned from sales copy

innovative solutions cutting-edge world-class synergy best-in-class empowering leverage seamless disruptive

Replace each with a specific claim and proof.

Exercises

  1. Headline drill. Pick a product. Write 25 headlines using different formulas. Pick the top 3 with a friend.
  2. PAS one-pager. Write a one-page PAS sales letter. Read it out loud — does it sound human?
  3. Email teardown. Subscribe to 5 brands you admire. Save their welcome sequences. What do they share?
  4. Voice audit. Write 100 words about something you care about. Then 100 words about a product. Bring them closer.
