06B2B Sales

Selling to businesses.

B2B isn't about convincing a buyer. It's about navigating complex organizational psychology to help a coalition of buyers convince each other internally—often without you in the room.

1. Academic Foundation: Transaction Cost Economics & Game Theory

Selling to a business is fundamentally different from selling to an individual. The framework for understanding B2B dynamics relies on Transaction Cost Economics (Williamson, 1981) and Cooperative Game Theory. In a B2B environment, organizations do not act as monolithic entities; they are networks of individual actors, each trying to minimize their personal transaction costs (effort, risk, and cognitive load) while maximizing their departmental status.

When a company makes a $1M software purchase, the "company" isn't buying anything. A coalition of humans is deciding to allocate resources. If the perceived risk of the transaction (a failed deployment, a data breach, a career-ending mistake) outweighs the collective payoff, the system defaults to the status quo.

2. The Buying Committee

In modern enterprise sales, the average buying committee consists of 6.8+ decision-makers. You are never selling to one person. You are managing a distributed consensus process.

Mobilizer (Champion)
Internal advocate with political capital. A standard 'Champion' just likes your product; a 'Mobilizer' can actually drive consensus across a conflicted buying committee and force a decision.
Economic Buyer
Controls the budget. VP, Director, CFO. Often only joins at the end to validate the financial ROI and negotiate terms.
End User
Will actually use the product. Opinion matters for adoption, but they often lack the authority to sign the contract.
Technical / Security
IT, security, legal. Their mandate is risk mitigation. They can kill a deal silently if their loss-aversion triggers are hit.
Procurement
Final negotiator. Their job is to squeeze you on price and protect the company's margin.
The Saboteur
Someone whose status or budget is threatened by your solution. Often invisible until they derail the deal.

3. Multithreading & Biological Alignment

Because the buying committee is a tribe of diverse individuals, they frequently operate in opposing biological states. Marketing might be in a Ventral Vagal state (excited for growth), while IT is in a Sympathetic state (anxious about implementation risk).

Multithreading is the practice of building relationships with multiple stakeholders simultaneously to manage these competing biological states. Single-threaded deals (where you only talk to the Mobilizer) are extremely fragile. If they lose political capital, your deal dies.

Elite sellers map the political hierarchy and execute targeted co-regulation for each stakeholder: they bring high energy to the End User, rigorous logic to the Economic Buyer, and extreme physiological grounding (safety) to the Technical/Security buyer.

4. Discovery Frameworks & Qualification

To navigate this complexity, elite reps rely on established frameworks to enforce discipline.

MEDDPICC

The enterprise standard for qualification. It answers the question: "Should I keep spending time on this deal?"

  • Metrics — what numerically improves if we win?
  • Economic Buyer — have I met the money person?
  • Decision Criteria — what will they decide on?
  • Decision Process — what's the path to signed?
  • Paper Process — legal, procurement, and vendor onboarding path.
  • Identify Pain — what hurts now? How much?
  • Champion — who internally is fighting for us?
  • Competition — including internal builds and "do nothing".

SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham, 1988)

A questioning framework based on actual research into what top reps do.

  • Situation — understand current state (sparingly)
  • Problem — uncover pains and dissatisfactions
  • Implication — explore what those problems cost them
  • Need-payoff — get the buyer to articulate the benefit

Challenger Sale

Top reps don't just relationship-build — they challenge the buyer's worldview.

  1. Teach — bring a unique insight about their business
  2. Tailor — adapt the insight to their situation
  3. Take control — push back on price, timeline, process

5. Clinical Case Studies

Case Study 1: The $1.2M SaaS Migration (Mobilizer vs Economic Buyer)

Background: A mid-market HR tech company was selling a complete platform overhaul to a logistics firm. The VP of HR (Mobilizer) loved the product and promised the deal was closed.

The Incident: The seller relied entirely on the VP of HR (single-threaded). When the proposal reached the CFO (Economic Buyer), it was immediately vetoed because the CFO's primary mandate was cash preservation, not HR efficiency.

The Fix: The seller initiated multithreading late, requesting a joint meeting. By applying Game Theory, the seller mapped the CFO's incentive structure and proved that the new platform would reduce compliance audit penalties by $400K annually—a metric the CFO cared about.

The Result: The CFO approved the $1.2M CapEx because the seller aligned the solution with the Economic Buyer's specific transaction costs, rather than relying on the Mobilizer's emotional excitement.

Case Study 2: The $3.5M Industrial Robotics CapEx (Saboteur Neutralization)

Background: An industrial robotics firm was pitching an automated sorting arm to a massive warehouse operator. The VP of Operations was eager to modernize.

The Incident: The Floor Manager (Saboteur) began actively blocking the deployment, fabricating safety concerns. The amateur rep tried to argue with the Floor Manager using data (logic).

The Biological Reality: The Floor Manager felt an intense status threat; the robots would make his manual scheduling expertise obsolete. His Sympathetic nervous system viewed the rep as a predator.

The Result: The elite rep co-regulated the Saboteur by offering him an exclusive certification in "Robotic Fleet Management," elevating his status from manual supervisor to tech manager. The Saboteur became a Co-Champion, and the $3.5M deal cleared.

Case Study 3: The $800K Enterprise Data Pipeline (Procurement Lock-Out)

Background: A data startup was at the finish line of an $800K deal. Procurement stepped in and demanded a 30% discount, citing a competitor's pricing.

The Incident: The startup's AE panicked (Fight/Flight) and offered 20% off. Procurement immediately demanded 25%.

The Re-Orientation: The AE realized they were playing Procurement's game. They paused, grounded themselves (Ventral Vagal), and multithreaded back to the CTO (Mobilizer): "Procurement is forcing us to strip out the real-time analytics module to meet their price. Do you want to proceed without real-time?"

The Result: The CTO, furious at losing critical features, bypassed Procurement and authorized the purchase at full price. The AE weaponized internal alignment to neutralize the Procurement threat.

6. Tactical Application: The Multithreading Pivot

When you are talking to a mid-level manager who cannot sign the contract, you must gently bypass them without triggering their ego (status threat).

Transcript Analysis

Mid-Level Manager (Proud): "I have the authority to make the technical recommendation for this project. If I say yes, we buy."

Amateur Seller (Sympathetic): "Great! But who actually signs the check? I need to talk to them." (Result: The manager's ego is crushed; they feel demoted and become a Saboteur.)

Elite Seller (Adult Ego State - Nurturing Tone): "That's fantastic. I know you've put a ton of work into evaluating this architecture. Usually, when I work with leaders like yourself on a project of this scale, the CFO eventually taps us on the shoulder to ask about the ROI model."

Elite Seller (3-second pause): "How do we make sure that when the CFO comes to you, you and I have a bulletproof business case ready for them?"

The Breakdown: The elite seller validates the manager's status ("leaders like yourself"), predicts the future obstacle (the CFO), and positions themselves as an ally helping the manager look good in front of their boss. They have successfully initiated multithreading while protecting the Mobilizer's ego.

7. Troubleshooting Pitfalls

In B2B enterprise sales, cognitive biases and biological reactions destroy deals:

  • Single-Threading (The Isolation Trap): Relying on one person to sell your product internally is a death sentence. If you haven't spoken to at least three people on the buying committee, you do not have a deal.
  • Happy Ears (Confirmation Bias): A rep hears "We love it!" from an End User and immediately forecasts the deal as 'Commit'. The rep ignores the fact that they haven't met the Economic Buyer and have no established Decision Process (failing MEDDPICC).
  • Pitching the Wrong Value: Pitching "ease of use" to a CFO, or pitching "ROI" to an End User. You must tailor the transaction cost and value proposition to the specific ego and biological needs of the stakeholder you are speaking to.
  • Fear of Procurement: Reps view Procurement as a terrifying authority figure (Parent ego state) and instantly regress into an Adapted Child posture, giving away margins without fighting. Procurement is just doing their job; stay in your Adult ego state.
  • Ignoring the Paper Process: A verbal "yes" means nothing if legal takes 60 days to redline the MSA. Elite reps run the 'Paper Process' in parallel with the technical evaluation to compress the sales cycle.
