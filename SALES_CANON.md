# Sales Canon — Curated, Classified, and Evidence-Aware

This is a curriculum map, not a claim that every book is empirically validated. Books are
classified by their best use and by the evidence class defined in `RESEARCH.md`.

## How to read anything on this list

For any work classed **F** (framework) or **V** (vendor dataset), write down before you
finish it: *what would I observe in my own deals if this were false?* Then look. A framework
that survives that test in your context is worth adopting; one that cannot be tested at all
should be held as vocabulary rather than as method.

The rule matters because several works below are excellent, influential, and asserting more
than their evidence supports — and reading them uniformly is how folklore enters practice.

## Research foundations (class R)

- **Verbeke, Dietz & Verwaal (2011)** — the contemporary sales-performance meta-analysis. The
  single most important paper on this list: selling-related knowledge (β = .28), adaptiveness
  (β = .27), and role ambiguity (β = −.25) top the hierarchy, with personality far below.
- **Churchill, Ford, Hartley & Walker (1985)** — the classic performance meta-analysis the
  above revises.
- **Franke & Park** — adaptive-selling meta-analysis.
- **Schmidt, Oh & Shaffer (2016)** — 100 years of selection-methods research. Governs
  everything the course says about hiring; cite the 2016 update, not the 1998 figures.
- *Organizational Buying Behavior* — **Webster & Wind**. The conceptual foundation for buying
  centres. Pre-digital: the roles hold, the information environment does not.
- *Influence* — **Robert Cialdini**. Research-informed, but the principles replicate
  unevenly — social proof strongly, scarcity modestly, unity least tested. Read alongside
  Module 02's grading.
- *Thinking, Fast and Slow* — **Daniel Kahneman**. Essential history and concepts. Several
  cited studies did not survive replication, notably in the priming chapters; the core
  prospect-theory work stands.
- *The Trusted Advisor* — **Maister, Green & Galford**. Class **F**. A heuristic, not a
  measured model, and unusually good at diagnosing why a specific relationship is stuck.

## Discovery and consultative selling

- [*SPIN Selling* — Neil Rackham](https://www.routledge.com/SPIN--Selling/Rackham/p/book/9780566076893). Large practitioner research program; strongest use is complex discovery practice.
- *Let’s Get Real or Let’s Not Play* — Mahan Khalsa & Randy Illig. Mutual diagnosis and decision.
- *Gap Selling* — Keenan. Current-state/future-state practitioner framework.
- *The Lost Art of Closing* — Anthony Iannarino. Commitment sequence; use as practice, not universal law.
- *Question-Based Selling* — Thomas Freese. Question design; test locally.

## Strategy, accounts, and complex buying

- [*The New Strategic Selling* — Miller, Heiman & Tuleja](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/robert-b-miller/the-new-strategic-selling/9780446695190/). Stakeholder and account-planning vocabulary.
- *Major Account Sales Strategy* — Neil Rackham. Complex-sale application.
- [*The Challenger Sale*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/309666/the-challenger-sale-by-matthew-dixon/) and [*The Challenger Customer*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/318682/the-challenger-customer-by-brent-adamson-matthew-dixon-pat-spenner-and-nick-toman/). Practitioner/vendor research; use teaching, tailoring, mobilization, and consensus as hypotheses.
- [*The JOLT Effect*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/697165/the-jolt-effect-by-matthew-dixon-and-ted-mckenna/). Practitioner research on indecision; do not universalize vendor-derived rates.
- *MEDDICC* — Andy Whyte. Qualification vocabulary; use as an evidence checklist.
- *The Qualified Sales Leader* — John McMahon. Enterprise inspection and coaching practice.
- *Dealstorming* — Tim Sanders. Cross-functional pursuit practice.

## Customer research, positioning, and founder selling

- [*Competing Against Luck* / Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/). Useful customer-progress lens; not a complete market-research method.
- *The Mom Test* — Rob Fitzpatrick. Practitioner interview discipline.
- [*Obviously Awesome* — April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/books). Practical B2B positioning sequence.
- *Positioning* — Al Ries & Jack Trout. Influential category framing; dated examples require updating.
- [*Founding Sales* — Pete Kazanjy](https://www.foundingsales.com/). Open practitioner handbook for early B2B selling.
- *Crossing the Chasm* — Geoffrey Moore. Market adoption strategy; use carefully outside technology markets.

## Value, negotiation, pricing, and decisions

- [*Getting to Yes* — Fisher, Ury & Patton](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/324551/getting-to-yes-by-roger-fisher-and-william-ury/). Interests, alternatives, options, and standards.
- *Negotiation Genius* — Malhotra & Bazerman. Research-informed preparation and decision traps.
- *Never Split the Difference* — Chris Voss. Memorable tactical tools; treat anecdotes and universal claims cautiously.
- *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing* — Nagle, Hogan & Zale. Pricing foundations.
- *Monetizing Innovation* — Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke. Willingness-to-pay and packaging practice.
- *Value Merchants* — Anderson, Narus & van Rossum. B2B value evidence and quantified propositions.
- *Financial Intelligence* — Berman, Knight & Case. Accessible commercial-finance grounding.

## Prospecting, messaging, and demos

- *Fanatical Prospecting* — Jeb Blount. Activity and channel practice; benchmarks are context-specific.
- *New Sales. Simplified.* — Mike Weinberg. Prospecting operating discipline.
- *Predictable Revenue* — Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler. Historically important specialization model; inspect current fit and handoff costs.
- *The Sales Development Playbook* — Trish Bertuzzi. SDR design and execution.
- *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* — Joseph Sugarman; *Breakthrough Advertising* — Eugene Schwartz; *Ogilvy on Advertising* — David Ogilvy. Craft references, not controlled evidence.
- *Demonstrating to Win!* — Robert Riefstahl. Demo planning framework.

## Management, enablement, and revenue systems

- [*The Sales Acceleration Formula* — Mark Roberge](https://newsroom.wiley.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2015/Mark-Roberge-Chief-Revenue-Officer-Hubspot-Details-Strategies-and-Secrets-of-the-Sales-Acceleration-Formula/default.aspx). Measurement-oriented practitioner system from HubSpot.
- *Cracking the Sales Management Code* — Jason Jordan & Michelle Vazzana. Controllable activities and outcomes.
- *Coaching Salespeople into Sales Champions* — Keith Rosen. Coaching practice; pair with training research.
- *The Sales Manager’s Guide to Greatness* — Kevin Davis. Frontline management practice.
- *Sales Management. Simplified.* — Mike Weinberg. Management operating discipline.
- *Revenue Operations* — Stephen Diorio & Chris Hummel. Cross-functional revenue architecture.
- *Measure What Matters* — John Doerr. Goal-system context; not sales-specific.

## Retention, customer success, and partnerships

- *Customer Success* — Mehta, Steinman & Murphy. Customer-success operating model.
- *Farm Don’t Hunt* — Guy Nirpaz. Expansion and customer value practice.
- *The Alliance* — Hoffman, Casnocha & Yeh. Employment relationship context.
- *Partnernomics* — Mark Brigman. Partnership practice; validate models against relationship and channel research.

## Use with caution — and the specific reasons

- **NLP sales books (class X).** The core claims have been tested repeatedly and have not
  survived. Witkowski's review of 315 NLP publications found roughly 18% supportive; Sturt
  et al. found 89% of 33 health-outcome studies lacked control groups; Sharpley's review of
  15 predicate-matching studies found correlations typically below .30 and no stable
  representational system. Module 22 salvages the four practices worth keeping and explains
  why the false mechanism makes sellers worse.
- **"Brain selling," "reptilian brain," and polyvagal explanations (class X on mechanism).**
  Polyvagal Theory's specific physiological claims are disputed in the primary literature
  (Grossman, 2023). The practical advice attached to them is usually sound for ordinary
  reasons; take the advice, drop the neuroscience (Module 28).
- **Books promising guaranteed closes or universal conversion ratios.** Any effect quoted as
  a clean constant — "it takes eight touches", "losses hurt twice as much" — is almost
  always an artifact. See the calibration rules in `RESEARCH.md`.
- **Personality typologies used to diagnose customers from brief behavior.** No validated
  instrument supports this, and the practical cost is that a seller who has classified a
  buyer stops asking questions.
- **Popular evolutionary-psychology sales writing.** The field is legitimate; its commercial
  adaptation is largely unfalsifiable storytelling, and it slides readily into
  group-level generalizations that are unsupported and, in hiring or customer-treatment
  decisions, frequently unlawful (Module 30).
- **Anecdote-only books** presenting exceptional wins without base rates, losses, or
  selection effects.

## Read critically rather than avoided

These are on the syllabus precisely because learners meet them presented as fact:

- *The Challenger Sale* — **Dixon & Adamson**. Class **V/F**. The teaching posture and
  constructive tension are genuinely valuable; the profile taxonomy rests on vendor survey
  research with contested methodology and has not independently replicated.
- *The JOLT Effect* — **Dixon & McKenna**. Class **V/F**. The best available treatment of
  buyer indecision and the mechanism most consistent with the wider decision literature.
  Proprietary dataset; hold the percentages loosely and use the mechanism.
- *Games People Play* — **Eric Berne**. Class **F**. Systematic reviews find a small,
  methodologically weak literature. Useful as vocabulary for interaction patterns; useless
  and inappropriate as diagnosis (Module 27).
- *Crossing the Chasm* — **Geoffrey Moore**. Class **F**. A narrative model for framing
  segment sequencing, not a validated theory of adoption.
- **René Girard on mimetic desire.** Class **F**, interpretive. The commercially useful
  residue — references work by identification rather than by impressiveness — is better
  supported by conformity research and institutional isomorphism (Module 20).

## Reading rule

For every book, record: the job it helps perform, claims that are evidence-backed, claims that are practitioner hypotheses, context where it may fail, one practice artifact, and one outcome measure. Reading without retrieval, rehearsal, and field evidence is not mastery.
