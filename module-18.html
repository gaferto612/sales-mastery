18Sales Management

Leading the team.

The transition from top-performing rep to sales manager is notoriously difficult. What made you successful as an individual contributor will not make you successful as a leader.

The IC to Manager Transition

The biggest mistake new sales managers make is trying to be "Super Reps". They jump on their reps' calls, take over the negotiation, and close the deal themselves.

The Super Rep Trap

If you close your reps' deals for them, they learn nothing, they feel disempowered, and you become the bottleneck for your entire team's revenue.

As an Individual Contributor (IC), your job is to win deals. As a Manager, your job is to build people who win deals. Your success is no longer measured by your close rate, but by the aggregate success of your team.

Coaching vs. Managing

Managing is about numbers (pipeline, activity, quotas). Coaching is about skills (discovery, objection handling, tone). You must do both, but separately.

The Pipeline Review (Managing)

  • Focus: The Deals
  • Cadence: Weekly (30 mins)
  • Questions: "What is the next step for this deal?", "Why is this stuck in stage 3?", "Do we have access to power here?"
  • Goal: Forecasting accuracy and deal strategy.

The 1-on-1 (Coaching)

  • Focus: The Person
  • Cadence: Weekly or Bi-Weekly (45 mins)
  • Questions: "Listen to this call recording—what could you have asked differently here?", "Where do you want your career to be in 2 years?"
  • Goal: Skill development and retention.

Pipeline & Forecasting

A sales manager's most critical deliverable to the CEO/Board is an accurate forecast. If you miss your forecast constantly, you will be fired.

  • Commit: Deals that are 90%+ likely to close. You bet your job on these.
  • Best Case: Deals that have a clear path to close this quarter, but risk remains.
  • Pipeline: Early stage deals.

Good managers rigorously interrogate their reps' deals. They look for Happy Ears (reps who hear what they want to hear) and demand objective evidence (e.g., MEDDIC criteria) before allowing a deal into the "Commit" category.

Hiring Elite Reps

A bad hire costs a company hundreds of thousands of dollars in wasted base salary, burned leads, and missed quotas.

Look for these 4 traits when interviewing:

  1. Coachability: Do a mock pitch in the interview. Give them harsh feedback. Ask them to do it again. Did they apply the feedback, or get defensive?
  2. Curiosity: Elite sellers ask deep questions. Do they ask you incisive questions about your company's product, market, and challenges?
  3. Resilience: Ask about a time they failed spectacularly and what they learned. Sales is a profession of rejection.
  4. Intelligence/Business Acumen: Can they understand complex business problems and articulate them clearly?

Building Sales Culture

Sales culture is not ping-pong tables and beer kegs. A winning sales culture is built on:

  • Transparency: Everyone's numbers are public. Celebrate the wins, analyze the losses without shame.
  • Psychological Safety: Reps must feel safe bringing bad news to their manager early. "Bad news early is good news."
  • Continuous Learning: Weekly film reviews (listening to call recordings together), roleplaying difficult scenarios, and sharing what's working across the team.
