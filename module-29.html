29The OODA Loop

Cognitive agility.

Developed by military strategist John Boyd, the OODA Loop is the ultimate framework for outmaneuvering competition in chaotic, high-stakes environments. In sales, whoever cycles through the loop fastest controls the reality of the deal.

15 min read Military Strategy · Competitive Intel Masterclass
In this module
  • Observe
  • Orient (The Core)
  • Decide & Act
  • Disrupting the Competition

1. Observe

The loop begins with gathering raw data. In sales, this is deep discovery and competitive intelligence. You must observe the buyer's environment, their budget constraints, the internal political dynamics of their 6.8+ buying committee, and which competitors are currently in the mix.

Amateurs stop observing once they find a problem their product can solve. Elite sellers never stop observing, continuously updating their data as the deal evolves.

2. Orient (The Most Critical Phase)

Orientation is how you filter the raw data through your mental models. John Boyd called this the "Schwerpunkt" (the focal point). If your orientation is flawed (e.g., "They just care about price"), your decisions and actions will fail.

A buyer might say, "We need a faster reporting tool." An amateur orients to: "They need speed. I will pitch our fast software." An elite seller orients to: "Why do they need speed right now? Because the new CFO is terrified of missing the Q3 board deadline and looking incompetent."

Correct orientation connects the technical symptom to the underlying emotional and political reality.

3. Decide & 4. Act

Once you are correctly oriented, you generate hypotheses (Decide) and test them (Act). Your action might be a specific closing question, a tailored demo, or introducing a Mutual Action Plan.

Crucially, every Action immediately generates new data, forcing you back to the Observe phase. The loop is continuous.

Disrupting the Competition's Loop

The true power of the OODA Loop in competitive deals is using it defensively to break your competitor's loop. If you can change the buyer's criteria faster than your competitor can react, the competitor will be acting on outdated information.

Setting the Trap

If you know your competitor struggles with SOC2 compliance, you educate the buyer on why SOC2 is the only legal way to proceed in 2026. You alter the buyer's Orientation. When your competitor shows up to pitch features, they are completely blindsided by a barrage of security questions they aren't prepared for. You broke their OODA Loop.
