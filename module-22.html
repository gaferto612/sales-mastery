Matching & Mirroring

Humans are biologically hardwired to trust people who are similar to them. Mirroring is the subtle, subconscious replication of another person's non-verbal signals, vocal cadence, and vocabulary.

  • Vocal Mirroring: If a prospect speaks slowly and deliberately, and you respond with rapid-fire enthusiasm, you break rapport. Match their speed, volume, and rhythm.
  • Chris Voss Style: Repeat the last 1-3 words of what the prospect just said with an upward, inquisitive inflection. It triggers the prospect to keep talking and reveals deeper information without you having to ask probing questions.

Sensory Predicates

People process the world through different primary sensory systems (Representational Systems): Visual, Auditory, or Kinesthetic.

Listen for the Predicates

If a buyer says, "I can't quite picture how this works," they are Visual. If you reply, "Let me talk you through it" (Auditory), you miss the connection. Instead, say, "Let me show you the blueprint."

Matching your language to their primary representational system makes your message fundamentally easier for their brain to process.

Future Pacing

Future Pacing is a technique that forces the buyer's brain to simulate a successful outcome. The brain struggles to differentiate between a vividly imagined future and reality.

Instead of saying, "If you buy our software, you will save time," you say: "Imagine it's six months from now. You walk into the board meeting. You pull up this dashboard, and instead of spending the whole weekend compiling the report, the data is just sitting there. How does that change the conversation you have with your CEO?"

You are moving them past the decision to buy, directly into the emotional payoff of the result.

Identity Anchoring

People make decisions based on who they believe they are (or want to be). Identity is the strongest lever of influence.

When you say, "Most forward-thinking innovators we work with approach the problem this way," you are associating your solution with the identity of a "forward-thinking innovator." If the prospect views themselves (or wishes to be viewed) as an innovator, rejecting your proposal creates cognitive dissonance with their own identity.

You are not aligning with their logic. You are aligning with their ego.
