Keeping what you won.

If you're spending most of your time acquiring and very little keeping, you've built a leaky bucket — and you're fighting math.

Retention is where the actual business lives. Acquisition just pays for the introduction.

The numbers you need to know

LTV — Lifetime Value

Total revenue from a customer over their entire relationship.

LTV = Avg revenue/month × Avg lifetime in months

Or for non-subscription: LTV = Avg order value × Repeat purchases × Margin %

CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost

CAC = Total sales + marketing ÷ New customers

LTV : CAC ratio

  • < 1 : losing money on every customer
  • 1–3 : tight, hard to scale
  • 3+ : healthy
  • 5+ : excellent (or undermarketed)

Churn rate

Churn = Customers lost ÷ Total customers (in a period)

5% monthly churn = nearly 50% annual churn. The math compounds against you brutally.

NRR — Net Revenue Retention

For subscription businesses, the single most important metric.

NRR = (Start MRR + Expansion − Churn − Downgrades) ÷ Start MRR

NRR > 100% means your existing customers grow you even with zero new customers. That's the dream.

The 5 pillars of customer retention

01.

Deliver on the promise (immediately)

The first 30 days set the entire relationship. Onboarding is not paperwork — it's the second sale.

  • Get them to "first value" as fast as possible
  • Proactive welcome — don't make them search
  • Quick wins in the first session
  • Clear documentation, video walkthroughs
  • Real human check-in within 7 days
02.

Stay valuable over time

  • Ship improvements visibly
  • Educational content keeps them growing
  • Regular check-ins, not just at renewal time
  • Customer-only perks: early access, community, exclusive content
03.

Make problems easy to solve

Support isn't overhead — it's retention. One bad support experience can erase years of goodwill.

  • Fast response times (in the medium they prefer)
  • Empower agents to actually solve, not just escalate
  • Track NPS and CSAT continuously
  • Treat complaints as gifts — they're the ones who didn't leave silently
04.

Build emotional connection

  • Remember details that matter to them
  • Celebrate their milestones, not just yours
  • Apologize properly and fast when you fail
  • Surprise them occasionally — a card, an upgrade, recognition
05.

Expand the relationship

Retention isn't just keeping — it's growing. Existing customers buy more from you than new ones, if you give them reasons.

  • Upsells: more value, higher tier
  • Cross-sells: adjacent products
  • Multi-year contracts: lock in price, ease their planning

Onboarding sequence — first 30 days

Standardize this. Top-performing companies onboard with the same care they sell with.

Day 0

Welcome — set the tone

Immediate thank-you (real, not "your purchase has been received"). Confirm what they bought, what happens next, who to contact.

Day 1

First-value action

Their first meaningful win with the product. Make it dead simple. Track who completes it — that's your activation metric.

Day 3–7

Human check-in

Real person reaches out. "How's it going? Any questions?" Don't pitch upgrades yet.

Day 14

Education push

Send tutorials for the next-level features they haven't discovered. Each email = one feature.

Day 30

Outcome check

"Are you getting what you hoped for?" If yes — ask for testimonial or referral. If no — engage support, save the relationship.

Win-back: when they leave

Customers churn. The question is what you do next.

Exit interview

Every churned customer gets a real conversation, not a survey. Ask:

  • What's the actual reason you're leaving?
  • Was there a specific moment things went wrong?
  • What would have changed your mind?
  • Would you come back if [specific change]?

Win-back campaign

3–6 months later, reach out. Things often change:

  • Their situation may have shifted
  • You may have shipped the feature they needed
  • The competitor they switched to may have disappointed
Honest reach-out

"Hi [Name], it's been 6 months since you left us. I wanted to honestly check in — are things working with [Competitor]? We've made some changes you might find interesting. Worth a quick chat?"

Turning customers into advocates

Your best customers should sell for you. Build the systems:

  • Referral programs — incentivize for both giver and receiver
  • Testimonial requests — at peak satisfaction moments (right after a win, not in a generic email)
  • Case studies — co-created, makes them look good too
  • Community — give them somewhere to meet other customers
  • VIP / power user programs — recognize the top 5%
  • Public recognition — feature them on social, in case studies

Exercises

  1. LTV calculation. Use the calculator above with real numbers from your business or one you can access. What's the ratio? Where's the leverage?
  2. Onboarding audit. Walk through the first 30 days of being a new customer somewhere you bought recently. What was good? Bad? Missing?
  3. Exit interview practice. Talk to a customer (or pretend) who left a service. Ask the 4 questions above. Notice the patterns.
  4. Referral ask. Pick 3 happy customers. Ask each, in a personal way, who else might benefit. Track results.
