The online selling landscape

Online selling spans a dozen distinct business models. Each has different mechanics, different metrics, different traps. Start with one channel until you can do it well, then expand. Five mediocre channels lose to one excellent channel.

Own store
Brand-building

Shopify, WooCommerce. Your site, your rules. But traffic is your problem.

Marketplaces
Volume

Amazon, eBay, Etsy. Built-in traffic, but platform owns the customer.

Social commerce
Impulse

TikTok Shop, IG Shopping. Where attention lives. Algorithm-dependent.

Dropshipping
Razor margins

Low capital. Quality and shipping issues are constant.

Digital products
High margin

Courses, ebooks, SaaS. 90%+ margins. Distribution is the game.

Subscriptions
Predictable

Recurring revenue. Churn is fatal — every month is renewal day.

High-ticket coaching
Quick start

You're the product. Doesn't scale without systems.

Affiliate
No asset

Sell others' products. No long-term asset, platform risk.

Anatomy of a converting product page

Every product page answers five buyer questions, in order. If any is missing or weak, the sale leaks out.

Q1
What is this?

Above the fold: clear hero image, one-line value proposition, price visible, CTA button without scrolling.

Q2
Why should I care?

Benefits section: top 3–5 outcomes. Outcome-led copy ("Wake up rested" > "8-zone memory foam"). Lifestyle imagery.

Q3
How does it work?

Features mapped to benefits. Specs, dimensions, materials. Comparison vs. alternatives if relevant.

Q4
Can I trust this?

Reviews (with photos/video), star ratings, press mentions, certifications, founder story, return policy.

Q5
What if it doesn't work?

Risk reversal: money-back, free returns, warranty. FAQ on top fears. Clear shipping and support info.

Golden rule

LTV must be at least 3× CAC for a healthy long-term business. If you spend €30 acquiring a customer worth €40 lifetime, you're working for nothing.

Where traffic actually comes from

Paid traffic — renting attention

  • Meta ads — visual consumer products, interest targeting
  • Google ads — high-intent (people already searching)
  • TikTok ads — impulse, trends, younger demos
  • YouTube ads — considered purchases needing demo

The moment you stop paying, it stops.

Owned traffic — owning attention

  • SEO / content marketing — slow but compounding
  • Email list — highest-ROI channel in online business
  • SMS list — even higher engagement; use sparingly

The hardest to build, the most valuable to have.

Earned traffic — receiving attention

  • Word of mouth — most powerful, hardest to manufacture
  • Press & PR
  • Influencer collaborations
  • UGC — one viral TikTok beats most ads
Use paid to bootstrap. Build email/SMS lists from day one. Work toward earned over time.

The conversion formula

Revenue = Traffic × CR × AOV × Repeat

If any one of these doubles, revenue doubles. Most beginners obsess over traffic. Operators know the leverage is usually in conversion rate or AOV.

Common online selling traps

  1. Building before validating — making the product before proving anyone will buy
  2. Chasing trends — by the time it's on YouTube, the window's closing
  3. Cheap traffic, expensive funnel — if your page converts at 0.3%, more traffic won't save you
  4. Ignoring email — leaving 30%+ of revenue on the table
  5. Discounting reflexively — trains buyers to wait for sales
  6. Underpricing — low price signals low quality
  7. Treating support as overhead — it's your highest-leverage retention channel
  8. Not knowing the numbers — running without metrics is gambling

Exercises

  1. Tear down 3 product pages. Map each to the five buyer questions. Where strong? Where weak?
  2. Calculate the funnel. Use the calculator above with real numbers from a store you can access.
  3. Pick your channel. Which one would you go all-in on for 90 days?
  4. Write a product page for an imaginary product hitting all 5 questions. Friend test it.
