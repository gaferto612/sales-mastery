What selling actually is

Selling is the process of helping another person decide to exchange something they have (usually money, time, or attention) for something you have (a product, service, or idea) such that both parties end up better off.

Notice what this rules out:

  • Tricking someone — that's fraud
  • Pressuring someone past their interest — that's manipulation
  • Hiding information — that's deception

And what it requires:

  • Genuine value on your side
  • Genuine need or want on theirs
  • A bridge of communication between the two

If any of those three are missing, you're not selling. You're either failing or harming.

Features → Benefits → Outcomes

This is the single most important distinction in selling. Most salespeople sell features. Good ones sell benefits. Great ones sell outcomes.

Feature
A factual attribute of the product
"18-volt lithium battery, ½-inch chuck"
Benefit
What that does for the user
"Drills through hardwood without slowing down"
Outcome
The change in the user's life
"Your shelves are up by Saturday and your spouse is impressed"
Drill

Take any product near you and write all three levels for it. If you can't, you don't yet know how to sell it.

The buyer's awareness journey

Buyers don't go from "never heard of you" to "give me your credit card" in one leap. They move through stages — and your message must match the stage.

Pitching product features to someone who doesn't know they have a problem will fail. Educating someone already ready to buy is wasted breath.

Price vs. value

Price is the number on the tag. Value is what the buyer believes they get for that number.

A €5,000 watch is "expensive" if value = telling the time. The same watch is "cheap" if value = signaling status to peers worth €50,000 in deals.

Your job is not to drop the price — it's to raise the perceived value until value > price in the buyer's mind. When that gap is wide enough, the sale happens.

The four levers of value

i.
Outcome
How big is the dream they get?
ii.
Likelihood
How sure are they they'll get it?
iii.
Time
How fast does the dream arrive?
iv.
Effort
How easy is it for them?

Improve any of these four and value goes up without changing the product.

The three currencies buyers spend

Money is the obvious one. The other two are often more precious.

Respect all three. Wasting a buyer's time or attention is how you lose them before they ever consider spending money.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Talking more than listening
  • Pitching before discovering
  • Selling features instead of outcomes
  • Competing on price instead of value
  • Trying to convince instead of qualifying out
  • Assuming all buyers are the same

Exercises

  1. Pick a product you own. Write its features, benefits, and outcomes. Show a friend and ask which level lands strongest for them.
  2. Audit a recent sales conversation (as buyer or seller). Map it onto the 7-step process. Where did it break down?
  3. Find an ad and identify which awareness level it's targeting. Is the targeting correct?
