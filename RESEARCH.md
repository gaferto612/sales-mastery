# Research standard

The course now distinguishes three kinds of statement:

1. **Research finding** — linked to a primary paper, systematic review, meta-analysis, law, regulator, or official professional guidance.
2. **Practice framework** — a tool for organizing work, not a universal law or guaranteed script.
3. **Illustrative scenario** — a fictional or composite example used for practice. Its numbers are assumptions, not benchmarks.

Conversion rates, margins, retention rates, talk ratios and other operating metrics must be measured in the learner's context. A benchmark is useful only when its population, period, sample, channel and denominator are disclosed.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer systematic reviews and meta-analyses; primary peer-reviewed research; official legislation and regulator guidance; government procurement guidance; and company filings only for company-specific metric definitions. Label practitioner models as frameworks.

Avoid unsourced viral statistics, neurological explanations inferred from behavior, deterministic personality labels, fabricated case studies, and universal claims based on vendor datasets.

## Maintenance rule

Every substantial factual revision should record its source in `research-data.js` or this bibliography. Review legal and regulatory links at least annually because requirements differ by jurisdiction and change over time.
