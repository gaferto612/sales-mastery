09Closing Techniques

Asking for the decision.

The close is not a moment — it's the natural conclusion of a conversation done well. If you've done the previous steps right, closing is just clarifying the next step.

The closing mindset

Most failed closes aren't tactical failures — they're conviction failures. If you secretly think your product isn't worth the price, the buyer hears it.

  1. Believe in the offer. If you don't think they should buy, why are you asking?
  2. The close is a service. You're helping them make a decision they're already considering.
  3. "No" is fine. A confident close that gets a no is better than a wishy-washy close that gets a "maybe" you'll chase for 3 months.
Beginners are afraid to ask for the close. Pros realize the close is what the buyer is waiting for.

When to close

Stop pitching the moment you see a buying signal. Continuing after the buyer is sold is the #1 way deals are lost.

  • They start asking implementation questions
  • They switch from "if" to "when"
  • They ask about pricing details, delivery, guarantee
  • They reference future use ("when we have this...")
  • They go quiet and look like they're thinking

When these appear: trial close, then full close.

Trial closes (test the temperature)

  • "How does this sound so far?"
  • "If we could start next week, would that work for your timeline?"
  • "Based on what we've discussed, does this seem like a fit?"

The actual ask

After the trial close gets a green light, ask for the decision. Then shut up.

Whoever speaks first after "Are you ready to move forward?" usually loses. If you fill the silence, you'll often re-open a settled deal or offer a concession that wasn't needed.

Drill

Ask the close, count to 10 silently before speaking again. It feels like an eternity. It's not.

The 17 closes

Below are the most-used closes across selling. Some are powerful; some are dated; some are bordering on manipulative. Match the close to the context. Click a card for an example script.

01
Direct Close
When · obvious fit, mature buyer
Just ask. "Want to move forward?" The most underused close in sales.
Are you ready to move forward?
02
Assumptive Close
When · strong buying signals shown
Skip to logistics. Assumes they've decided.
Great — should we start the paperwork, or do you want to walk through it once more?
03
Alternative Close
When · multiple options exist
Offer two yeses instead of yes/no. Assumes the buy.
Would you prefer the annual or monthly plan?
04
Summary Close
When · complex, multi-feature deals
Recap the value, then ask. Makes the yes feel earned.
So we've agreed it'll save 10 hours a week, integrate with your stack, and ROI in 6 months. Want to start?
05
Trial Close
When · testing temperature
Soft probe before the real ask.
If we could start Monday, would that work?
06
Question Close
When · they're 80% there
Ask what's left between them and yes.
Is there anything else you need to know before we proceed?
07
Silent Close
When · price is on the table
State the offer, go silent. Pressure of silence does the work.
The total is €4,800. [Wait.]
08
Puppy Dog Close
When · physical or trial product
Let them try it. Endowment effect closes for you.
Take it home for a week. If you don't love it, bring it back.
09
Takeaway Close
When · they're being indecisive
Suggest they're not a fit. Often triggers them to convince you.
Honestly — maybe this isn't the right fit for you. Should we hold off?
10
Ben Franklin Close
When · analytical buyers
Write pros and cons together. Helps logical decision-making.
Let's write down the reasons for and against — together.
11
Scarcity Close
When · genuinely limited only
Use only when scarcity is real. Fake scarcity destroys trust.
We have two spots left in this cohort. Should I save one?
12
Urgency Close
When · genuine deadline exists
Real time pressure works. Manufactured urgency feels gross fast.
The Q4 pricing ends Friday. Worth deciding before then?
13
Next-Step Close
When · long B2B cycles
Not "buy now" — "what's the next step?" Always have one.
What's the right next step from here?
14
1-2-3 Close
When · summarizing simple offer
Brain loves threes. Recap in 3 benefits, then ask.
So: saves time, saves money, removes risk. Ready?
15
Empathy Close
When · sensitive or emotional buyers
Acknowledge how they're feeling, then ask.
I can see this is a big decision. What feels right?
16
Conditional Close
When · objections remain
"If I can solve X, will you buy?" — surfaces real blockers.
If we could include onboarding at no extra cost, would you sign today?
17
Walk-Away Close
When · the deal isn't a fit
Honestly walking away. Builds enormous long-term trust. Often closes anyway.
I don't think we're the right fit. Let me suggest someone who might be.

Which close fits which situation?

Situation Best close Why
Simple consumer purchase Assumptive, Alternative Reduces decision friction
Long B2B cycle Summary, Next-Step Anchors agreement, advances deal
Analytical buyer Ben Franklin, Summary Respects their decision style
Indecisive prospect Takeaway, Direct Forces a decision, removes "maybe"
High-emotion (real estate, weddings) Empathy, Puppy Dog Validates emotion, lets product sell itself
Price objection lingering Conditional Surfaces real blocker, trades cleanly
Genuine deadline exists Urgency, Scarcity Truthful time pressure works
Not a fit Walk-Away Right thing to do, plus often closes
Ethics watch

Closes 11 (scarcity) and 12 (urgency) work only when they're true. Fake scarcity creates short-term sales and long-term distrust. If a customer ever finds out the deadline was manufactured, you've lost them and their referrals forever.

After the close

The moment after "yes" is when most reps mess up.

  • Stop selling. Don't keep extolling features. Confirm and move to logistics.
  • Reduce buyer's remorse. Briefly reinforce they made a great choice. Specific, not generic.
  • Set expectations. What happens next: delivery, onboarding, first invoice, etc.
  • Ask for referrals if appropriate. Buyers feel best about you right after the close.
  • Follow through faster than they expect. Onboarding speed sets the tone for the whole relationship.

Common closing mistakes

  • Never actually asking for the sale
  • Asking and then immediately speaking again (the silent close fail)
  • Continuing to pitch after the buyer is sold
  • Closing too early (before discovery is done)
  • Closing too late (buyer has cooled)
  • Using high-pressure tactics on uncertain buyers
  • Conceding without trading
  • Forgetting to confirm the next step

Exercises

  1. Close inventory. Pick 5 of the 17 closes that match your context. Write the exact script for each — in your own voice.
  2. Silent close drill. Today: in one conversation, state a price/proposal, then count 10 seconds silently. Track what happens.
  3. Trial close drill. Before any full close this week, use a trial close first. Notice the difference.
  4. Buying-signal log. For one week, log every buying signal you notice in any sales conversation (yours or others). Build the pattern recognition.
