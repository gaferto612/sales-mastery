17Product-Led Sales

The hybrid model.

Historically, you either had a massive sales team dialing for dollars, or you relied entirely on a self-serve checkout page. Today, the most successful companies bridge the gap with Product-Led Sales (PLS) — using behavioral data to know exactly who to call, and exactly when to call them.

The end of the cold call

In traditional SaaS, an SDR builds a list of prospects based on job titles and blasts them with cold emails. The conversion rate is abysmal because there is zero intent.

In Product-Led Growth (PLG), the user signs up for a free tool (like Slack, Notion, or Figma) and starts using it. The product is the primary driver of acquisition.

Product-Led Sales (PLS) is the connective tissue. It means the sales team no longer looks at static lists of names; they look at dashboards of product usage. The "Cold Call" is dead. Every call is now a "Warm Call" backed by behavioral data.

Product Qualified Leads (PQLs)

A Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL) is someone who downloaded a whitepaper. A Product Qualified Lead (PQL) is someone who has experienced actual value in your product.

You cannot scale PLS without defining what triggers an intervention. These triggers are usually based on Activation Milestones:

  • Usage Thresholds: A user hits 80% of their free storage limit.
  • Collaboration Hooks: A user invites 3 teammates to a workspace.
  • High-Value Features: A user clicks on the "SSO Integration" tab (indicating they are likely an enterprise user).
Rule of Thumb

If your sales team reaches out before the user has hit their "Aha!" moment in the product, you are just doing annoying traditional outbound.

The 5 PLS Motions

A modern PLS playbook categorizes outreach into five distinct motions:

  1. Assist: Reaching out to a new sign-up who is struggling to complete onboarding or integrate a data source. The goal is not to sell, but to unblock them.
  2. Convert: Contacting a highly active free user who has hit specific milestones, guiding them to a paid self-serve tier.
  3. Expand: Identifying power users within an existing paid account and selling them more seats or a higher-tier add-on.
  4. Consolidate: The holy grail of PLS. Noticing that 40 different employees at a large company are using personal credit cards to pay for your tool, and reaching out to the CIO to sell a centralized Enterprise contract.
  5. Defend: Proactively reaching out to accounts whose usage has dropped 30% week-over-week to prevent churn.

Value-First Outreach

Because you have data, you must never send a generic "Just checking in" email.

Bad: "Hi, I saw you signed up for our tool. Do you want to jump on a 15-minute call to see a demo?"

Good: "Hi, I noticed you successfully invited two teammates to your workspace yesterday, but haven't set up the Slack integration yet. I recorded a quick 60-second Loom video showing you how to do it. Let me know if you want help setting it up for your team."

The goal of PLS outreach is to act as a highly specialized consultant who guides the user to the next logical step of value.
