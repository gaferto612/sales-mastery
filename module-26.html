26The Inner Game

Stoicism & dopamine.

Sales has one of the highest burnout rates of any profession. The constant rejection, the end-of-quarter pressure, and the euphoria of a closed deal create a toxic neurochemical rollercoaster. Elite performance requires mastering your own mind.

The Dopamine Trap

When you close a deal, your brain releases a massive spike of dopamine. When a deal falls through, dopamine crashes, leading to anxiety, lethargy, and call reluctance. If you allow your neurochemistry to be dictated by your win rate, you will burn out within two years.

Elite sellers shift their dopamine release from the outcome (closing the deal) to the process (executing the perfect discovery call, rigidly adhering to MEDDPICC). By rewarding yourself for controlling what you can control, you build sustainable motivation.

Sales Stoicism

Stoicism is the ancient philosophy of differentiating between what is within your control and what is not. In enterprise sales, this is a superpower.

  • Not in your control: The buyer's budget getting cut, the economic climate, a competitor dropping their price by 50%.
  • In your control: The quality of your discovery, your daily prospecting volume, your emotional regulation on a call.

When a €500k deal dies because the sponsor was unexpectedly fired, the amateur seller spirals into despair. The Stoic seller accepts the reality immediately, closes the CRM record, and opens a new prospecting sequence.

Outcome Detachment

Buyers can smell desperation. If you need the deal to hit your quota or pay your mortgage, your voice pitch rises, you talk too much, and you concede too early.

The Paradox of Sales

The less you need the deal, the more likely you are to win it.

Outcome detachment means entering a negotiation fully prepared to walk away. This creates an aura of authority and abundance. When you genuinely believe, "I would love to help you, but if you don't buy this, my company and my life will be perfectly fine," the buyer feels the shift in power dynamics and will naturally gravitate toward you.

Defending Deep Work

The modern seller's brain is fractured by Slack notifications, CRM alerts, and endless internal meetings. You cannot execute complex enterprise strategy if your attention span is 40 seconds.

Elite performers ruthlessly defend blocks of "Deep Work." This means turning off all notifications for 90-minute sprints to conduct account research, write highly personalized copy, or map out a Mutual Action Plan. Do not confuse frantic responsiveness with actual productivity.
