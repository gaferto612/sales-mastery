08Objection Handling

The seven universal "no"s.

Most objections aren't roadblocks — they're invitations. The buyer is telling you exactly what's standing between them and yes. Your job is to listen to what they said, not what you wish they said.

Why objections exist

An objection means the buyer is still engaged. They haven't walked away. They're telling you what they need before they can say yes.

Most beginners hate objections. Pros welcome them. The deals that close silently with no objections are often the ones that ghost you next week.

An objection is not a rejection. It's a request for more information.

Real vs. smokescreen objections

The biggest mistake in objection handling is taking the first objection at face value. Often it's a polite cover for the real concern.

Smokescreen — what they say

  • "It's too expensive"
  • "I need to think about it"
  • "Send me more info"
  • "I'll get back to you"
  • "Let me check with my partner"

Real — what they mean

  • "I don't see the value"
  • "I'm not sure this works"
  • "I want to avoid saying no to your face"
  • "I'm not the decision-maker"
  • "I'm afraid of making a mistake"
Diagnostic

When you get an objection, ask: "Is that the main thing, or is there something else?" Often the second answer is the real one.

The universal 5-step framework

Apply this to any objection. Following the order matters — skipping steps is how objections come back.

Step 01
Acknowledge

Show you heard them. Don't dismiss, don't immediately counter. "I hear you" or "That makes sense" — even just nodding. The buyer needs to feel heard before they can listen.

Step 02
Clarify

Get specific. "When you say expensive — expensive compared to what?" or "What part specifically concerns you?" Most objections are vague; clarification reveals the real one.

Step 03
Empathize

Show you understand. "I get why that matters — most of our customers asked the same thing before they bought." This normalizes the concern.

Step 04
Respond

Address the actual concern with specifics — reframe, evidence, comparison, story, or trade-off. Not a pitch dump.

Step 05
Confirm

Check: "Does that address your concern, or is there something else?" If yes, move forward. If no, you missed it — go back to step 2.

The seven universal objections

Across every industry and channel, almost every "no" reduces to one of these seven. Click to see the breakdown for each.

Often a smokescreen ~40% of all objections

What they probably mean: "I don't see enough value to justify this price."

Clarifying question

Expensive compared to what — another option, or just more than you hoped?

Response options

  • Reframe to value: "If this saved you 10 hours a week, what would that be worth?"
  • Break it down: "At €2,400/year that's €6.50/day — less than coffee."
  • Comparison: "Compared to the cost of not solving this — what's that costing you currently?"
  • Trade: "I can do €X if you commit to two years" — never discount without exchange
Half real, half smokescreen

What they probably mean: "I'm not convinced enough to prioritize this over other things."

Clarifying question

What would need to be true for the timing to be right?

Response options

  • Cost of delay: "What does waiting 6 months cost you?"
  • Set future trigger: "When should I check back? What will be different then?"
  • Calendar it: "Let's put it on the calendar for 3 months — that way it doesn't fall off your radar."
Often legitimate

What they probably mean: Either really true (someone else decides) or a polite stall.

Clarifying question

Of course. Who needs to be involved, and what do they typically care about?

Response options

  • Offer a meeting with the other person
  • Provide materials they can share with the decision-maker
  • Ask: "What would you say if you were in my position?" — often reveals if it's a stall
  • Anticipate: "What questions do you think they'll have?"
Critical

Qualify the decision-making process before the pitch. If you find out someone else decides during objection handling, you wasted the pitch.

Often legitimate Sometimes you should agree

What they probably mean: Genuine concern about applicability to their specific situation.

Clarifying question

What specifically makes you think it might not fit?

Response options

  • Find a customer like them, share that story
  • Ask about their situation — match features to specifics
  • Suggest a small trial or proof-of-concept
  • Sometimes: Agree it's not a fit. Builds enormous trust for the future.

What they probably mean: "Convince me you're better, or just give me your best price."

Clarifying question

That's a great company. What's drawing you to them — and what's making you still consider us?

Response options

  • Never trash competitors — sounds insecure
  • Acknowledge their strengths, then differentiate
  • Find the criteria they care about, address those
  • If they're truly better for this case, say so

What they probably mean: "I'm afraid of making a mistake I'll get blamed for."

Response options

  • Case studies and testimonials
  • References they can call
  • Free trial / pilot / money-back
  • Data: numbers, results, third-party validation
  • Risk reversal: "If this doesn't deliver X in 90 days, full refund."

Trust objections often hide a "fear of looking stupid" — make the decision safe for them personally.

Often legitimate

What they probably mean: They either truly don't need it (qualify out — save your time) or haven't realized they do.

Clarifying question

Tell me about how you're handling [problem] today.

Response options

  • Re-do discovery — find the actual pain
  • Use SPIN's implication questions to amplify pain awareness
  • If pain is genuinely absent, qualify out and move on

Preventing objections before they arrive

The best objection handling is making them unnecessary. Address them upstream:

  • Discovery — uncover budget, authority, timing, fit early so they never become surprises
  • Social proof — show case studies that match their situation
  • Pre-frame — "A lot of customers wonder if this would fit a small team. Here's what we typically see..."
  • FAQ in advance — answer the top 5 objections in your sales page or pitch
  • Risk reversal — guarantees that pre-empt the trust objection
Pro move

List your objections, then answer each in your pitch. If you handled "price" before they brought it up, they often don't bring it up at all.

Exercises

  1. Top 5 objection log. Write down the 5 most common objections you (or your industry) hear. Script the 5-step response for each.
  2. Real vs. fake drill. Take 5 recent objections you've heard. For each, write the surface objection and the likely real one.
  3. Role-play. Partner up. They lob random objections from the 7 categories. You respond with the framework. 15 min.
  4. Pre-empt audit. Take your current pitch / sales page. Which of the 7 objections does it address upstream? Add one more.
