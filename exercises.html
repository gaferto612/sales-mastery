—Exercises & Drills

Knowledge without reps is theater.

Twenty short, repeatable drills tied to the modules. Solo or with a partner. Recording yourself creates useful evidence for reflection and feedback. Open each drill below for the prompt and scoring.

How to use this section

  • Solo: read the prompt, set a timer, write or speak the answer. Review honestly.
  • With a partner: trade roles (seller / buyer). Switch after each round.
  • In a group / class: rotate observers who give feedback after each round.
  • Recurring: the best drills get repeated weekly or monthly. Skills decay without use.
Read the book → close zero deals. Run the drills → close real ones.

Interactive Templates & Matrices

Use these fill-in-the-blank structures to build your own scripts before running the drills.

Subject: [Observation about their company, e.g., 'New office in Berlin']

Line 1 (Context): Saw that you just [Trigger event].

Line 2 (Problem): Usually when companies do that, they run into [Specific Pain Point] around month 3.

Line 3 (Solution/Value): We help [Role]s avoid that by [One sentence on how you solve it].

Line 4 (Ask): Worth a brief chat to see if this is relevant for you right now?

Pattern Interrupt: "Hi [Name], I'll be honest, I know you weren't expecting my call. Do you have 30 seconds for me to explain why I called, and then you can hang up if it's not relevant?"

The Hook: "I'm calling because I saw [Trigger]. Most [Role]s I speak to tell me they are struggling with [Pain 1] or [Pain 2]. Are you dealing with either of those right now?"

Objection Acknowledge & Mirror Isolate & Pivot
"It's too expensive." "Too expensive?" (Pause) "Aside from price, is there any other reason we wouldn't move forward?"
"We use [Competitor]." "You use [Competitor]. That makes sense." "What's one thing you wish they did better?"
"Send me an email." "I can absolutely do that." "Just so I don't send you generic marketing fluff, what specifically should I include?"

The 20 drills

Module 15 min prep · 30s delivery

Objective: Articulate product value concisely without jargon for distinct audiences within 30 seconds.

Scenario: You meet someone in an elevator who asks "What do you do?" — explain what you sell in 30 seconds so a 10-year-old would get it, and then so a busy corporate CEO would care.

Inputs: Core value proposition statement, target buyer profile, and a 30-second timer.

Rubric: Jargon-free language (30%); outcome-focused message (30%); time compliance under 30s (20%); listener engagement and follow-up pull (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Rehearse pitch with a peer partner and time each other using a stopwatch.
• Advanced: Deliver the pitch to a skeptical buyer who interrupts with "Why should I care?" after 10 seconds.

Module 115 min

Objective: Translate technical product features into customer benefits and measurable real-life business outcomes.

Scenario: Select 3 everyday or software products near you. For each item, convert specs into user benefits and business outcomes.

Inputs: Product feature list, buyer persona matrix, and 3-column conversion table (Feature | Benefit | Outcome).

Rubric: Feature distinction (25%); benefit connection (30%); outcome impact (30%); clarity and audience resonance (15%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Analyze 3 physical consumer products (e.g., standing desk, noise-canceling headphones).
• Advanced: Analyze 3 complex enterprise SaaS capabilities and map them to financial executive metrics (EBITDA, CAC, churn).

Module 230 min

Objective: Audit sales collateral against Cialdini's 7 principles of persuasion to evaluate ethical influence mechanics.

Scenario: Analyze a recent sales page, ad campaign, or cold email to identify implemented and missing influence principles.

Inputs: Target sales asset, Cialdini 7-principles checklist (Reciprocity, Commitment, Social Proof, Authority, Liking, Scarcity, Unity).

Rubric: Principle identification accuracy (35%); critique of execution quality (35%); ethical improvement suggestions (30%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Audit a single B2C landing page for 3 prominent principles.
• Advanced: Audit a multi-touch B2B outbound campaign and add missing principles without using artificial scarcity or false social proof.

Modules 9, 33Ongoing · 1 week

Objective: Practice tactical empathy by labeling observed buyer emotions in real time to defuse tension and deepen discovery.

Scenario: During professional conversations, observe non-verbal cues or tone shifts and verbally label the underlying emotion.

Inputs: Verbal label prompt bank ("It seems like...", "It sounds like...", "It looks like..."), active call recording or conversation environment.

Rubric: Label timing and tone (30%); avoidance of self-referential language ("I hear you") (30%); silence post-label (20%); depth of buyer elaboration (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Apply one emotion label per conversation in daily internal discussions.
• Advanced: Execute empathy labeling during high-friction price negotiation or objection pushback.

Module 3330 min

Objective: Formulate and refine a high-leverage question sequence using the SPIN framework to guide discovery.

Scenario: Prepare a discovery call structure for a buyer considering replacing an legacy workflow solution.

Inputs: Target customer persona, SPIN question matrix template, 20 draft discovery questions.

Rubric: SPIN category alignment (30%); curation down to top 10 questions (30%); buyer-centric tone vs interrogation (25%); diagnostic clarity (15%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Draft 5 Situation and 5 Problem questions for a familiar product.
• Advanced: Draft 5 Implication and 5 Need-Payoff questions uncovering hidden operational risks and executive financial impacts.

Module 10Ongoing · 1 week

Objective: Master conversational silence following a direct ask or closing statement to give the buyer room to commit.

Scenario: Delivering a pricing proposal or closing request during a live sales interaction.

Inputs: Propose next step or commercial term script, 7-second internal counting technique.

Rubric: Maintaining 7 seconds of absolute silence (40%); calm posture and vocal poise (30%); quality of buyer response (30%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Practice the 7-second silent count in peer role-plays after asking "How would you like to proceed?".
• Advanced: Execute the silent close in live prospect calls immediately following commercial terms or discount requests.

Module 945 min

Objective: Build a structured objection handling repository applying a 5-step response framework.

Scenario: Facing the top 10 most frequent objections encountered in your target market.

Inputs: Top 10 market objection list, 5-step response framework template (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond, Confirm).

Rubric: Framework step adherence (35%); objection root cause diagnosis (25%); conversational delivery (25%); quarterly refresh cadence (15%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Script responses for the 3 most common price and timing objections.
• Advanced: Script and rehearse responses for complex competitive, security, and status-quo pushbacks.

Modules 8, 9Ongoing · 1 week

Objective: Combine verbal mirroring with emotional labeling to extract underlying buyer constraints.

Scenario: A prospect gives a guarded or ambiguous objection during a negotiation call.

Inputs: Mirroring rule (repeat last 3 words as a question), label prompt list, pause protocol.

Rubric: Mirror accuracy and inflection (30%); pause execution (30%); label follow-up precision (25%); information extracted (15%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Practice mirroring 3 buyer statements in peer role-plays without adding commentary.
• Advanced: Combine mirroring, a 4-second pause, and an emotional label during live price negotiation pushback.

All modules60 min

Objective: Analyze a recorded sales presentation to evaluate process execution, persuasive mechanics, and closing effectiveness.

Scenario: Reviewing a recorded sales pitch, demo video, or call recording.

Inputs: Call recording or transcript, 7-step sales process map, awareness level framework, Cialdini checklist.

Rubric: Stage mapping accuracy (30%); identification of buyer awareness stage (25%); objection handling assessment (25%); actionable takeaways (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Evaluate a 5-minute product presentation video for core sales process steps.
• Advanced: Conduct a complete 45-minute enterprise call teardown with talk-ratio analysis and question scoring.

Module 830 min

Objective: Construct a comprehensive negotiation matrix defining positions, concessions, and walk-away bounds.

Scenario: Preparing commercial parameters for an active enterprise or high-value deal.

Inputs: Negotiation planner template (BATNA, Reservation Price, Target Price, ZOPA, 5 Tradeable Non-Price Variables).

Rubric: Parameter clarity (30%); tradeable variable creativity (30%); realistic BATNA definition (20%); strategic concession sequence (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Define BATNA, target price, and reservation price for a single-product transaction.
• Advanced: Build a multi-variable enterprise negotiation matrix incorporating term length, payment terms, case study rights, and SLA scope.

Modules 5, 7, 3245 min

Objective: Draft and test three cold outreach emails focused on personal context, core pain, and curiosity angles.

Scenario: Outbound prospecting sequence targeting a specific executive decision-maker.

Inputs: Prospect profile data, 3 verified trigger facts, cold email structure template (Context, Problem, Value, Ask).

Rubric: Brevity under 100 words (25%); clear angle differentiation (30%); absence of spam triggers (25%); peer review vote score (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Draft 3 email variants targeting a department lead.
• Advanced: Write a 3-touch multi-channel sequence combining email, LinkedIn message, and voicemail scripts.

Module 730 min

Objective: Generate 25 subject lines or headlines across different formulas to identify high-performing hooks.

Scenario: Creating outreach hooks or landing page headlines for a campaign launch.

Inputs: Headline formula matrix (How-To, Direct Pain, Curiosity, Proof-based, Pattern Interrupt), product value proposition.

Rubric: Formula diversity (30%); elimination of weak hooks (30%); test feedback gathering (20%); selection logic for top 5 (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Write 10 subject lines for a single email campaign and select the top 2.
• Advanced: Draft 25 headlines across 5 distinct buyer personas and execute a live split-test.

Modules 8, 1210 min

Objective: Develop psychological comfort and verbal fluency when disqualifying non-fit opportunities or walking away from bad terms.

Scenario: Rehearsing vocal delivery when ending a call with an unqualified or unviable prospect.

Inputs: Disqualification scripts ("We are not the right fit...", "I recommend competitor X...", "Let's stop here...").

Rubric: Vocal firmness and calm tone (35%); non-defensive delivery (35%); clear referral or clean exit (30%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Rehearse 3 walk-away scripts out loud in front of a mirror until natural.
• Advanced: Execute a walk-away disqualification in a partner role-play when a prospect demands custom scope outside your product core.

Modules 8, 930 min · partner

Objective: Maintain composure, active listening, and constructive framing when handling aggressive or dismissive objections.

Scenario: A 5-minute role-play against a partner acting as a skeptical buyer pushing on price or past vendor failure.

Inputs: Hostile buyer objection prompts, non-defensive response frameworks.

Rubric: Emotional composure (30%); empathy label usage (30%); objection isolation (20%); constructive conversation pivot (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Role-play handling a competitor price objection with 1-minute time-out pauses.
• Advanced: Role-play an unscripted 10-minute high-pressure meeting with an aggressive procurement officer.

Module 415 min

Objective: Audit and adjust speaking pace, vocal tone, and filler word usage via audio recording analysis.

Scenario: Self-recording a 2-minute product pitch or discovery call introduction.

Inputs: Audio recorder, vocal evaluation metrics sheet (WPM pace, rising inflections, filler counts: um/like/you know).

Rubric: Pace control (120-150 WPM) (30%); zero filler words (30%); authoritative downward vocal inflection (25%); re-recording improvement (15%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Record a 60-second pitch and count filler words on playback.
• Advanced: Record a 15-minute simulated presentation and re-record until zero filler words and rising inflections remain.

Module 11Ongoing

Objective: Implement a repeatable customer referral request workflow following successful deal onboarding.

Scenario: Reaching out to recently onboarded, satisfied customers 1-2 weeks post-close.

Inputs: Closed-won customer roster, 2-step referral ask template (thank-you note + specific referral question).

Rubric: Follow-up timing (30%); clear non-pressuring request (30%); customer feedback responsiveness (20%); referral tracking discipline (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Send a structured referral email to 3 recent satisfied clients.
• Advanced: Integrate formal referral requests into quarterly account executive business reviews (QBRs).

Module 1390 days

Objective: Execute a focused 90-day deliberate practice routine on one target capability to achieve mastery.

Scenario: Selecting one target sales skill (e.g. discovery, objection handling, closing) for quarter-long deliberate practice.

Inputs: 90-day practice tracker, weekly recording log, peer feedback scorecard.

Rubric: Practice consistency (35%); recording review discipline (25%); peer feedback integration (20%); measurable skill growth (20%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Focus 90 days on mastering empathy labeling across all calls.
• Advanced: Focus 90 days on financial business case construction with peer review from a finance manager.

Module 3360 min · partner

Objective: Produce and evaluate a shared diagnosis rather than merely complete a question list.

Scenario: A 120-person logistics company is investigating recurring invoice disputes with conflicting departmental opinions.

Inputs: Preparation sheet (facts, assumptions, unknowns, no-fit hypothesis), 30-minute recorded role-play, causal problem map.

Rubric: Preparation and relevance (20%); listening evidence (30%); diagnostic accuracy (25%); uncertainty and ethics (15%); next-step quality (10%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Conduct a 15-minute discovery role-play focused on contracting, core symptom mapping, and summary confirmation.
• Advanced: Repeat discovery with conflicting finance and IT stakeholders and reconcile diagnostic discrepancies without assuming consensus.

Module 3145 min

Objective: Reconstruct a recent buying decision without defending the seller or turning one account into a market-wide conclusion.

Scenario: Interviewing a recent buyer, non-buyer, or churned customer strictly for learning purposes.

Inputs: Win/loss interview protocol (trigger, alternatives, stakeholder influence, friction points), decision mapping grid.

Rubric: Neutrality and non-defensiveness (25%); behavioral specificity (25%); traceability (20%); treatment of uncertainty (15%); decision relevance (15%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Conduct a 20-minute post-deal review interview with a friendly customer.
• Advanced: Compare three win/loss/churn decisions and preserve the outlier case that contradicts dominant assumptions.

All modules60 min · quarterly

Objective: Conduct a reflective quarterly assessment of sales skills, deal losses, and self-deceptions to set learning priorities.

Scenario: End-of-quarter performance and skill review session.

Inputs: 5 self-audit reflection questions (weakest module, avoided tasks, deal loss self-deceptions, peer feedback, next drill target).

Rubric: Honest self-assessment depth (35%); identification of deal loss root causes (35%); actionability of next quarter drill plan (30%).

Variations:
• Beginner: Answer the 5 self-audit questions in writing and identify 1 skill to practice.
• Advanced: Present quarterly self-audit findings and deal loss autopsies to a sales peer or manager for feedback.
