How to use this section

  • Solo: read the prompt, set a timer, write or speak the answer. Review honestly.
  • With a partner: trade roles (seller / buyer). Switch after each round.
  • In a group / class: rotate observers who give feedback after each round.
  • Recurring: the best drills get repeated weekly or monthly. Skills decay without use.
Reading concepts builds initial awareness; running deliberate reps builds observable skill in live sales conversations.

Interactive Templates & Matrices

Use these fill-in-the-blank structures to build your own scripts before running the drills.

Subject: [Observation about their company, e.g., 'New office in Berlin']

Line 1 (Context): Saw that you just [Trigger event].

Line 2 (Problem): Usually when companies do that, they run into [Specific Pain Point] around month 3.

Line 3 (Solution/Value): We help [Role]s avoid that by [One sentence on how you solve it].

Line 4 (Ask): Worth a brief chat to see if this is relevant for you right now?

Pattern Interrupt: "Hi [Name], I'll be honest, I know you weren't expecting my call. Do you have 30 seconds for me to explain why I called, and then you can hang up if it's not relevant?"

The Hook: "I'm calling because I saw [Trigger]. Most [Role]s I speak to tell me they are struggling with [Pain 1] or [Pain 2]. Are you dealing with either of those right now?"

Objection Acknowledge & Mirror Isolate & Pivot
"It's too expensive." "Too expensive?" (Pause) "Aside from price, is there any other reason we wouldn't move forward?"
"We use [Competitor]." "You use [Competitor]. That makes sense." "What's one thing you wish they did better?"
"Send me an email." "I can absolutely do that." "Just so I don't send you generic marketing fluff, what specifically should I include?"

Module 15 min prep · 30s delivery

Why this reinforces the module: Module 1 insists value is stated in the buyer’s units, not the product’s. Thirty seconds is the constraint that exposes whether you can actually do that: under time pressure, sellers fall back to feature lists, so the drill fails loudly when the translation was never there.

Skill trained: Executive Value Pitching & Value Translation

Objective: Articulate core product value concisely without jargon or buzzwords for distinct buyer audiences within 30 seconds.

Scenario: You meet a prospective buyer in an elevator or informal networking event who asks, "What does your company do?" You must deliver a clear 30-second value pitch adapted for a 10-year-old child's comprehension, followed by an executive-ready version for a CFO.

Inputs: Core value proposition matrix, target buyer profile, and a 30-second timer.

Constraints: Maximum 30 seconds (75 words); zero industry jargon (no "synergy", "seamless", "next-gen"); must state customer problem and economic result.

Instructions: Draft a 3-sentence script: Trigger Problem → Core Solution Mechanism → Quantifiable Business Outcome. Perform a jargon audit to strip abstract buzzwords. Record two 30-second delivery takes and measure your speaking rate (target 130–140 words per minute).

Required output: Two 30-second audio recordings (or written transcripts) and a 1-sentence value translation key.

Time estimate: 5 min prep · 5 min execution (10 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Jargon-free clarity (30%); outcome focus over feature specs (30%); time compliance under 30s (20%); conversational cadence and engagement (20%).

Common mistakes: Pitching product features instead of customer outcomes; rushing the pace to squeeze in extra text; using internal company acronyms; failing to end with an open question.

Beginner variation: Rehearse a single 30-second pitch with a peer partner using a stopwatch, focusing strictly on clear simple language.

Advanced variation: Deliver the pitch to a skeptical executive role-player who interrupts at second 10 with "Why should I care?" and re-anchor without losing composure.

Partial exemplar: "Most mid-market CFOs lose 15 hours a week tracking vendor invoices across legacy spreadsheets. We automate invoice reconciliation so finance teams close monthly books in 2 days instead of 14, cutting operational processing costs by 40%. Would it make sense to see how your team compares?"

Self-review questions: Did I state a clear problem before mentioning the product? Did I avoid all abstract buzzwords? Did I finish before the timer expired?

Related modules: Module 1 (Foundations of Selling), Module 7 (Copywriting & Marketing)

Module 115 min

Why this reinforces the module: Module 1’s feature → benefit → outcome ladder is easy to agree with and hard to execute. Filling all three columns for a product you really sell shows which rung you can only complete with adjectives — usually the outcome one.

Skill trained: Commercial Value Mapping & Benefit Translation

Objective: Translate technical product features into customer benefits and measurable financial or operational business outcomes.

Scenario: Select 3 products or enterprise software capabilities. For each capability, convert technical specifications into immediate user benefits and executive business outcomes.

Inputs: Product feature list, buyer persona matrix, and 3-column conversion table (Feature | Benefit | Outcome).

Constraints: Must cite at least one measurable business metric (e.g. EBITDA impact, CAC reduction, error rate drop) for every outcome column entry.

Instructions: List 3 distinct features. For each feature, answer "What does this allow the user to do?" (Benefit) and "What economic or strategic metric changes for the buyer's company?" (Outcome). Fill out the 3-column matrix.

Required output: Completed 3-column conversion matrix covering 3 features across 3 user personas.

Time estimate: 10 min mapping · 5 min review (15 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Feature distinction (25%); benefit connection (30%); outcome impact precision (30%); clarity and executive resonance (15%).

Common mistakes: Stopping at benefits without reaching financial outcomes; confusing product features with user outcomes; using vague metrics like "saves time" without quantifying hours or dollar value.

Beginner variation: Analyze 3 physical consumer products (e.g. noise-canceling headphones: Active noise cancellation → Quiet focus during travel → 2 extra billable work hours per flight).

Advanced variation: Analyze 3 complex enterprise B2B SaaS features and map them directly to executive CFO metrics (churn rate, payback period, working capital optimization).

Partial exemplar: "Feature: Real-time automated SOC-2 compliance monitoring → Benefit: Engineering teams spend zero hours manually compiling compliance audit logs → Outcome: Accelerates deal enterprise security review cycles by 18 days, reducing sales cycle length and lowering compliance overhead by $65,000 annually."

Self-review questions: Did every row reach a financial or operational outcome? Are the metrics specific and believable? Could an executive understand why this matters to their P&L statement?

Related modules: Module 1 (Foundations of Selling), Module 3 (Online Selling)

Module 230 min

Why this reinforces the module: Module 2 grades each influence principle by evidence class rather than handing you a uniform toolkit. Auditing your own live material applies that grading where the incentive to overclaim is strongest: your own copy.

Skill trained: Ethical Persuasion Audit & Behavioral Influence Analysis

Objective: Audit sales collateral against Cialdini's 7 principles of persuasion to evaluate ethical influence mechanics and eliminate coercive tactics.

Scenario: Audit a recent sales landing page, outbound campaign, or proposal document to identify implemented and missing influence principles.

Inputs: Target sales asset, Cialdini 7-principles checklist (Reciprocity, Commitment/Consistency, Social Proof, Authority, Liking, Scarcity, Unity).

Constraints: Must evaluate all 7 principles; audit must identify both positive implementations and unethical or manipulative abuses (e.g., fake countdown timers).

Instructions: Read the sales asset thoroughly. Score each of the 7 principles on a 1–5 scale based on presence and authenticity. Draft recommendations to add missing authentic principles without creating artificial pressure.

Required output: Written 1-page audit memo with principle scoring breakdown and 3 actionable improvement recommendations.

Time estimate: 15 min collateral review · 15 min memo drafting (30 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Principle identification accuracy (35%); critique of execution authenticity (35%); ethical improvement suggestions (30%).

Common mistakes: Confusing artificial scarcity with genuine urgency; relying exclusively on social proof while ignoring authority signals; ignoring ethical boundaries in persuasion mechanics.

Beginner variation: Audit a single B2C landing page for 3 prominent principles (Social Proof, Reciprocity, Scarcity).

Advanced variation: Audit a multi-touch enterprise outbound cadence, adding subtle Social Proof and Unity triggers while removing pushy pressure tactics.

Partial exemplar: "Audit of Outbound Email Cadence: Reciprocity is present via the free benchmark report offer. Social Proof is weak—mentions 'many clients' without naming peer role benchmarks. Scarcity was inappropriately forced via a fake 'offer expires Friday' claim. Recommendation: Replace artificial countdown with a peer case study (Social Proof + Authority) showing how a similar VP of Engineering reduced outage resolution time."

Self-review questions: Did I evaluate all 7 principles? Are all identified principles based on authentic evidence rather than artificial manipulation? How can ethical influence increase trust?

Related modules: Module 2 (Psychology of Selling), Module 7 (Copywriting & Marketing)

Modules 9, 33Ongoing · 1 week

Why this reinforces the module: Modules 9 and 33 both depend on the buyer continuing to talk after an uncomfortable moment. Labelling is the mechanic that buys that continuation, and drilling it in isolation is what keeps it from hardening into one scripted phrase.

Skill trained: Tactical Empathy & Emotional De-escalation

Objective: Practice tactical empathy by labeling observed buyer emotions in real time to defuse tension and uncover unstated discovery insights.

Scenario: During customer calls or internal role-plays, observe non-verbal cues, tone shifts, or hesitations, and verbally label the underlying emotion.

Inputs: Verbal label prompt bank ("It seems like...", "It sounds like...", "It looks like..."), active call recording or role-play partner environment.

Constraints: Zero self-referential "I" statements allowed (do NOT say "I hear you" or "I understand"); mandatory 4-second pause immediately after delivering the label.

Instructions: When the buyer expresses hesitation or frustration, select a label prompt ("It seems like you're concerned about implementation bandwidth"). Deliver the label in a calm, downward-inflecting voice. Pause completely for 4 seconds to let the buyer elaborate.

Required output: Audio log or transcript record of 5 live empathy labels delivered across customer interactions, including buyer responses.

Time estimate: 5 min daily log review across 1 week (25 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Label timing and vocal tone (30%); complete avoidance of self-referential 'I' phrasing (30%); strict execution of silence post-label (20%); depth of buyer elaboration unlocked (20%).

Common mistakes: Using self-referential statements ("I understand how you feel"); speaking immediately after the label instead of waiting in silence; inflecting upwards as if asking for validation.

Beginner variation: Apply one emotion label per conversation in daily internal team discussions to build natural delivery habits.

Advanced variation: Execute empathy labeling during high-friction pricing negotiations or when a buyer expresses intense frustration with a past vendor.

Partial exemplar: "Buyer: 'We tried a tool like this last year and it was a complete disaster for our team.' Seller: 'It seems like you're worried about getting burned by another vendor rollout.' [4-second pause] Buyer: 'Exactly. We spent $80k and nobody logged in after week three because onboard training was non-existent.'"

Self-review questions: Did I avoid saying 'I'? Did I remain completely silent after speaking the label? Did the buyer expand on their core emotional driver?

Related modules: Module 8 (Negotiation), Module 9 (Objection Handling), Module 33 (Consultative Discovery & Diagnosis)

Skill trained: Diagnostic Questioning & SPIN Sequence Structuring

Objective: Formulate and refine a high-leverage question sequence using the SPIN framework (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-Payoff) to guide discovery.

Scenario: Structure a discovery interview sequence for a VP of Operations considering replacing an outdated manual workflow solution.

Inputs: Target customer persona sheet, SPIN question framework matrix, draft list of 20 discovery questions.

Constraints: Maximum 10 final questions; no more than 2 Situation questions; at least 3 Implication questions quantifying financial/operational risk.

Instructions: Brainstorm 20 potential discovery questions. Categorize each under Situation, Problem, Implication, or Need-Payoff. Edit down to the top 10 questions, ensuring a natural conversational progression from surface symptoms to business consequences.

Required output: Curated 10-question discovery guide formatted in a logical SPIN progression table.

Time estimate: 15 min brainstorming · 15 min curation & editing (30 min total)

Evaluation rubric: SPIN category accuracy (30%); curation to top 10 high-leverage questions (30%); conversational tone vs interrogation style (25%); diagnostic clarity of business impact (15%).

Common mistakes: Over-indexing on low-value Situation questions that could be answered via pre-call research; rushing to solution pitches before exploring Implications; asking leading or closed yes/no questions.

Beginner variation: Draft 3 Situation and 3 Problem questions for a familiar product or service.

Advanced variation: Draft 5 Implication questions that expose hidden cross-departmental downstream costs and executive-level P&L risks.

Partial exemplar: "Implication Question: 'When manual data entry delays monthly reporting by 4 days, how does that lag impact your team's ability to reallocate regional inventory before weekend peak demand hit?'"

Self-review questions: Could any of my Situation questions have been researched online beforehand? Do my Implication questions help the buyer calculate the cost of doing nothing?

Related modules: Module 6 (B2B Sales), Module 33 (Consultative Discovery & Diagnosis)

Module 10Ongoing · 1 week

Why this reinforces the module: Module 10 argues that the ask must be followed by silence, because filling the pause is how sellers negotiate against themselves. The tolerance for that silence is physical, not intellectual, so it has to be rehearsed rather than understood.

Skill trained: Conversational Poise & Closing Discipline

Objective: Master conversational silence immediately following a direct ask or commercial closing proposal to give the buyer room to process and commit.

Scenario: Delivering a pricing proposal, discount trade-off ask, or contract commitment request during a live sales interaction.

Inputs: Specific closing ask script ("Based on our timeline, are you ready to approve the implementation schedule today?"), 7-second internal counting technique.

Constraints: Mandatory minimum 7 seconds of complete silence following the closing question; zero nervous gestures, throat clearing, or qualifying follow-up statements.

Instructions: State the closing question clearly with a calm, neutral tone. Immediately close your mouth and count slowly to 7 in your head (1-one-thousand, 2-one-thousand...). Wait for the buyer to speak first, regardless of how uncomfortable the silence feels.

Required output: Audio recording or role-play clip log demonstrating 3 closing asks followed by 7+ seconds of silence.

Time estimate: 5 min daily drill rehearsal across 1 week (25 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Maintaining 7+ seconds of uncompromised silence (40%); calm vocal posture and non-verbal confidence (30%); quality and honesty of buyer response (30%).

Common mistakes: Filling silence out of discomfort by adding qualifiers ("...or we could look at options next week"); lowering pricing before the buyer responds; showing visible tension or fidgeting.

Beginner variation: Practice the 7-second silent count in peer role-plays after asking "How would you like to proceed from here?".

Advanced variation: Execute the silent close in live prospect calls immediately following commercial terms presentation or after proposing a non-monetary trade for a discount request.

Partial exemplar: "Seller: 'To lock in the Q3 onboarding cohort, we need the signed agreement by Thursday. Shall we send the signature link to legal today?' [7 seconds of absolute silence] Buyer: 'Thursday works, but let me make sure our procurement lead is available to sign.'"

Self-review questions: Did I wait until the buyer spoke first? Did I avoid adding any softening language after my question? How did my internal discomfort compare to the actual result?

Related modules: Module 10 (Closing Techniques), Module 8 (Negotiation)

Module 945 min

Why this reinforces the module: Module 9 holds that objections repeat far more than sellers believe. Building the inventory converts a live improvisation problem into a preparation problem, and usually reveals that you face six recurring objections, not sixty.

Skill trained: Objection Diagnosis & Structured Response Engineering

Objective: Build a structured objection handling repository applying a 5-step response framework (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond, Confirm).

Scenario: Prepare responses for the top 10 most common objections encountered in your target market (e.g. price, timing, competitor lock-in, internal priorities).

Inputs: List of 10 market objections, 5-step response framework matrix, customer call transcripts.

Constraints: Every objection response must follow all 5 steps; no defensive arguments or direct contradiction of buyer claims allowed.

Instructions: Document the 10 objections. For each, script a response specifying: 1. Active Listening prompt, 2. Acknowledgment label, 3. Exploratory question, 4. Reframe/Evidence response, 5. Confirmation check ("Does that address your concern?").

Required output: Completed 10-objection response matrix with detailed scripts for all 5 framework steps.

Time estimate: 25 min matrix scripting · 20 min practice & refinement (45 min total)

Evaluation rubric: 5-step framework adherence (35%); root cause diagnosis accuracy (25%); natural conversational tone (25%); clear confirmation checks (15%).

Common mistakes: Arguing with the prospect; responding instantly without acknowledging feelings; failing to check if the response resolved the objection.

Beginner variation: Script responses for the 3 most frequent price and timing objections using the 5-step framework.

Advanced variation: Script and rehearse responses for complex enterprise pushbacks involving legal risk, multi-vendor lock-in, and legacy system integration failure.

Partial exemplar: "Objection: 'Your product is 30% more expensive than Competitor X.' Step 1 & 2: 'It sounds like budget efficiency is a major priority for your leadership team.' Step 3: 'What specific capabilities were included in Competitor X's quote?' Step 4: 'While their upfront license cost is lower, our platform includes automated data migration, which eliminates $40k in third-party professional services.' Step 5: 'Does looking at total cost of ownership change how you evaluate the price gap?'"

Self-review questions: Did I explore the root cause before offering a counter-argument? Did I ask for confirmation after presenting my evidence?

Related modules: Module 9 (Objection Handling), Module 8 (Negotiation)

Skill trained: Information Extraction & Non-Confrontational Probing

Objective: Combine verbal mirroring with emotional labeling to extract underlying buyer constraints and hidden decision criteria without causing defensiveness.

Scenario: A prospect gives a guarded or ambiguous objection during a discovery or negotiation call (e.g. "We need to think about it").

Inputs: Mirroring rule (repeat the last 1–3 critical words as an inquiring question), emotion label prompts, mandatory 4-second pause rule.

Constraints: Mirror must use exact buyer words; must be followed by a 4-second pause, then an emotion label, then another 4-second pause.

Instructions: When a prospect makes a guarded statement, repeat the key 1–3 words with an inquisitive pitch. Pause for 4 seconds. If the prospect elaboration is incomplete, deliver an emotion label ("It sounds like there's an internal constraint we haven't discussed"). Pause for another 4 seconds.

Required output: Audio recording or written log analyzing 3 live mirror & label combinations executed in sales calls.

Time estimate: 5 min daily reflection across 1 week (25 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Mirror accuracy and vocal inflection (30%); strict execution of pauses (30%); label follow-up precision (25%); depth of hidden information revealed (15%).

Common mistakes: Paraphrasing instead of using exact buyer words; speaking immediately after mirroring; sounding sarcastic or aggressive rather than curious.

Beginner variation: Practice mirroring 3 buyer statements in peer role-plays without adding any commentary.

Advanced variation: Execute a seamless mirror, 4-second pause, and emotional label sequence during intense price pushback or legal redline negotiations.

Partial exemplar: "Buyer: 'We like the platform, but the timing just isn't right for our team.' Seller: 'Timing isn't right?' [4-second pause] Buyer: 'Well, our IT department is tied up with an ERP migration until November.' Seller: 'It sounds like you're worried about overloading your technical team.' [4-second pause] Buyer: 'Exactly, we can't take on another heavy integration right now.'"

Self-review questions: Did I repeat exact words? Did I maintain patient pauses? Did the combination reveal the hidden barrier behind the surface objection?

Skill trained: Sales Call Analysis & Talk-Ratio Evaluation

Objective: Analyze a recorded sales presentation or call recording to evaluate process execution, persuasive mechanics, and closing effectiveness.

Scenario: Review a recorded 30-to-60 minute sales pitch or demo call (your own or a peer's) using a standardized evaluation framework.

Inputs: Call recording audio/video, transcript, 7-step sales process map, talk-ratio tracker, Cialdini persuasion audit sheet.

Constraints: Must measure exact seller-to-buyer talk ratio; must evaluate call against 5 distinct stages (Opener, Discovery, Value Match, Objection Handling, Next Steps).

Instructions: Watch/listen to the call recording. Calculate talk ratio (target: seller speaks < 45% of total call time). Mark timestamps for key transition points, missed buyer cues, and unaddressed objections. Write a 1-page structured teardown report.

Required output: Written 1-page pitch teardown memo detailing timestamped observations, talk-ratio metrics, and 3 specific corrective actions.

Time estimate: 40 min call review · 20 min report writing (60 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Process stage mapping accuracy (30%); talk-ratio and question quality analysis (25%); identification of missed buyer signals (25%); actionability of improvement plan (20%).

Common mistakes: Focusing on slide design rather than conversational dynamics; overestimating buyer engagement; ignoring monologue lengths (>90 seconds without a check-in question).

Beginner variation: Evaluate a 5-minute recorded product pitch video for basic structure and talk tempo.

Advanced variation: Perform a complete enterprise multi-stakeholder call teardown, analyzing power dynamics, champion engagement, and procurement risk signals.

Partial exemplar: "Call Audit Summary: Seller talk ratio was 62% (excessive). At minute 14:20, buyer stated 'We are concerned about security integration', but seller ignored cue and continued presenting feature slides for 8 minutes. Action Plan: 1. Pause after every slide section to ask a diagnostic check question. 2. Address security concerns immediately using an empathy label."

Self-review questions: Was the seller talk ratio under 45%? Where did the seller miss key buyer verbal cues? What specific question should have been asked instead?

Skill trained: Commercial Negotiation Planning & Concession Matrix Construction

Objective: Construct a comprehensive negotiation matrix defining commercial parameters, tradeable variables, reservation prices, and walk-away boundaries before entering high-stakes deal discussions.

Scenario: Prepare commercial parameters for an active enterprise deal where the buyer procurement team is requesting a 25% discount and extended payment terms.

Inputs: Negotiation planner template (BATNA, Reservation Price, Target Price, Zone of Possible Agreement [ZOPA], 5 Tradeable Non-Price Variables).

Constraints: Must define at least 5 non-monetary tradeable items (e.g., case study participation, multi-year term commitment, upfront annual payment, expanded user seats, co-marketing rights); zero un-traded price concessions allowed.

Instructions: 1. Identify your Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement (BATNA). 2. Establish your target price and hard reservation price. 3. List 5 non-price tradeable variables with estimated cost to you vs value to buyer. 4. Map out concession trade pairs ("If you agree to X, we can consider Y").

Required output: Completed negotiation planner matrix containing BATNA definition, price bounds, and 5 paired concession trade scripts.

Time estimate: 20 min matrix mapping · 10 min trade script rehearsal (30 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Parameter clarity and realistic bounds (30%); creativity of non-price tradeables (30%); clear BATNA definition (20%); strategic concession pairing logic (20%).

Common mistakes: Granting price discounts without getting a valuable trade in return; failing to establish a true walk-away price point; underestimating the value of non-monetary terms like reference rights or extended contracts.

Beginner variation: Define BATNA, target price, and reservation price for a single-product transaction with 2 simple tradeables.

Advanced variation: Construct a multi-variable enterprise matrix balancing contract duration, SLA terms, payment schedules, and custom feature scope against procurement discount demands.

Partial exemplar: "Concession Trade Pair: Buyer requests 15% price discount. Counter-Trade Script: 'We cannot reduce the annual license fee directly, but if your company is willing to commit to a 2-year agreement paid annually upfront and participate in a published case study, we can adjust the annual rate by 12% to reflect those commercial terms.'"

Self-review questions: Do I have a clear walk-away price? Have I paired every potential discount with a valuable commercial trade? Is my BATNA credible?

Related modules: Module 8 (Negotiation), Module 24 (Working with Procurement)

Skill trained: Outbound Email Copywriting & Multi-Angle Prospecting

Objective: Draft and test three cold outreach emails focused on distinct psychological angles (Observation/Trigger, Core Pain/Consequence, Social Proof/Peer Benchmark) targeting a single buyer persona.

Scenario: Create a 3-touch outbound prospecting cadence for a VP of Sales who has recently expanded their SDR team.

Inputs: Prospect LinkedIn profile data, trigger event facts, cold email structural framework (Trigger Context → Pain Hypothesis → Low-Friction Value Proposition → Soft Call-to-Action).

Constraints: Maximum 100 words per email; zero spam triggers ("free", "guaranteed", "secret"); subject lines under 5 words; must end with low-friction interest asks rather than demanding time on calendar.

Instructions: Draft Email 1 focusing on a specific trigger event (e.g. recent hiring announcement). Draft Email 2 focusing on an industry pain point and operational consequence. Draft Email 3 leveraging peer benchmark proof. Review all three against word-count limits.

Required output: Three complete cold email copy variations with corresponding subject lines and prospect trigger context notes.

Time estimate: 30 min drafting · 15 min peer critique & editing (45 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Word-count compliance under 100 words (25%); angle differentiation across emails (30%); absence of spam/salesy language (25%); quality of low-friction call-to-action (20%).

Common mistakes: Writing generic self-focused intro paragraphs ("My name is X and we are the leading provider..."); making high-friction call-to-action requests ("Do you have 30 minutes for a demo on Tuesday?"); writing emails over 120 words.

Beginner variation: Draft 3 email variants targeting a department manager using simple problem-solution angles.

Advanced variation: Build a 3-touch multi-channel sequence combining custom cold email copy, personalized LinkedIn voice notes, and phone call opener scripts.

Partial exemplar: "Subject: SDR onboarding lag. Hi [Name], Saw your team added 4 SDRs this month. Usually when sales teams scale reps that quickly, ramp time to first pipeline meeting stretches past 90 days due to inconsistent playbook coaching. We helped [Peer Company] cut SDR ramp time from 85 to 34 days without adding manager headcount. Worth checking out if this is on your radar for Q3?"

Self-review questions: Is every email under 100 words? Does the subject line look like an internal email from a colleague? Did I ask for interest rather than a 30-minute calendar commitment?

Skill trained: Hook Engineering & Headline Optimization

Objective: Generate 25 subject lines or headlines across 5 distinct formula categories to identify high-converting hooks for outbound campaigns.

Scenario: Brainstorm subject lines and email hooks for an upcoming cold outreach campaign promoting a sales workflow automation tool.

Inputs: Headline formula taxonomy (1. Pattern Interrupt, 2. Direct Pain, 3. Curiosity / Open Loop, 4. Proof / Metric Benchmark, 5. Personal Trigger Observation).

Constraints: Must generate exactly 25 headlines (5 distinct entries for each of the 5 formula categories); maximum 6 words per subject line; zero clickbait false promises.

Instructions: Review the 5 formula categories. Write 5 unique subject line variations under each formula. Review the full list of 25, highlight the top 3 strongest options based on peer feedback or split-test predictions, and justify your selection.

Required output: Categorized list of 25 subject lines with highlighted top 3 picks and split-test hypothesis rationale.

Time estimate: 20 min headline generation · 10 min selection & review (30 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Formula category diversity (30%); hook quality and brevity under 6 words (30%); elimination of spam/clickbait triggers (20%); selection logic for top 3 options (20%).

Common mistakes: Using ALL CAPS or excessive punctuation (!!!); writing overly long subject lines that get truncated on mobile devices; relying on deceptive subject lines ("Re: Our meeting yesterday" when no prior contact exists).

Beginner variation: Write 10 subject lines across 2 formula categories for a single email campaign and select the top 2.

Advanced variation: Draft 25 subject lines across 5 distinct buyer personas and launch a live A/B test across a 500-prospect target segment.

Partial exemplar: "Pattern Interrupt: 'quick question about churn' | Direct Pain: '45-day pipeline stagnation' | Curiosity: 'strange discovery call metric' | Metric Benchmark: '34% lower CAC benchmark' | Personal Trigger: 'saw your Q2 expansion post'"

Self-review questions: Did I write 5 entries for all 5 formulas? Are all subject lines under 6 words? Would I open these emails if they landed in my own inbox?

Skill trained: Qualification Firmness & Disqualification Delivery

Objective: Develop psychological comfort and professional vocal fluency when disqualifying non-fit prospective clients or walking away from unviable commercial terms.

Scenario: Rehearse vocal delivery when terminating a sales discussion with a prospect demanding custom features outside your product roadmap or demanding an unviable price point.

Inputs: Disqualification script options ("Based on your requirements, we are not the right solution for your team...", "To deliver that price point, we would have to compromise quality, so we must respectfully pass...").

Constraints: Zero defensive or apologetic language (do NOT say "I'm sorry"); calm, neutral, authoritative tone; must offer a clean alternative recommendation or referral where appropriate.

Instructions: Stand or sit with relaxed posture. Deliver 3 distinct walk-away scripts out loud into an audio recorder. Re-listen to evaluate vocal firmness, eliminating trailing inflections or hesitation pauses.

Required output: Audio recording of 3 walk-away delivery variations demonstrating calm, non-apologetic authority.

Time estimate: 3 min script setup · 7 min audio recording & review (10 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Vocal firmness and matter-of-fact tone (35%); absolute absence of apologetic or defensive language (35%); clear professional referral or clean exit posture (30%).

Common mistakes: Apologizing for non-fit ("I'm so sorry we can't do that"); lingering in hope when a deal is clearly unviable; showing frustration or anger when walking away.

Beginner variation: Rehearse 3 walk-away scripts out loud in front of a mirror until vocal delivery feels smooth and effortless.

Advanced variation: Execute a walk-away disqualification in a live role-play against a demanding buyer who threatens to choose a competitor unless you yield to unreasonable demands.

Partial exemplar: "Seller: 'Based on your requirement for custom on-premise deployment, our cloud-native platform is not the right fit for your infrastructure. Module 415 min

Why this reinforces the module: Module 4 covers the channel where delivery carries information the transcript does not. Recording is the only feedback loop available for pace, because you cannot hear your own tempo while you are producing it.

Skill trained: Vocal Dynamics & Delivery Auditing

Objective: Audit and systematically adjust speaking pace, vocal tone inflection, and filler word frequency through audio recording and transcript analysis.

Scenario: Record a 2-minute simulated sales introduction or product demo summary, then analyze and re-record to achieve optimal vocal clarity and gravitas.

Inputs: Audio recording application, 2-minute pitch script, vocal audit score sheet (metrics: Words Per Minute, filler word tally [um, like, you know, sort of], ending pitch direction).

Constraints: Speaking rate must fall strictly between 120 and 150 words per minute; zero rising pitch inflections ("uptalk") at statement endings; zero filler words in re-recording.

Instructions: 1. Record Take 1 reading a 2-minute pitch script naturally. 2. Play back audio, counting total words and filler occurrences, noting rising inflections. 3. Practice applying downward inflection at sentence ends. 4. Record Take 2 and compare metrics against Take 1.

Required output: Two audio recordings (Take 1 baseline vs Take 2 optimized) and completed vocal audit metric log.

Time estimate: 5 min initial recording · 5 min playback audit · 5 min optimized re-recording (15 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Speaking pace control (120–150 WPM) (30%); filler word reduction/elimination (30%); authoritative downward vocal inflection (25%); measurable improvement from Take 1 to Take 2 (15%).

Common mistakes: Speaking too fast (>170 WPM) due to nervousness; using uptalk at sentence ends, which signals uncertainty; ignoring subtle filler words like 'right?' or 'so'.

Beginner variation: Record a 60-second pitch script, listen back, and count total filler words with a tally counter.

Advanced variation: Record a 15-minute simulated presentation and re-record until filler words and rising inflections are systematically minimized below baseline thresholds.

Partial exemplar: "Vocal Audit Log: Take 1: 168 WPM (too fast), 9 filler words ('um' x4, 'you know' x3, 'like' x2), 5 uptalk inflections. Take 2: 134 WPM (optimal), 0 filler words, 100% downward authoritative statement endings."

Self-review questions: Did my speaking speed stay between 120 and 150 WPM? Did I eliminate uptalk at sentence endings? Did Take 2 sound more authoritative than Take 1?

Related modules: Module 4 (Offline Selling), Module 6 (B2B Sales)

Instructions: Conduct a 30-minute role-play with a partner acting as buyer. Use open probing questions to uncover conflicting internal explanations. Draft a causal diagram mapping how operational breakdown leads to invoice disputes. Send a post-call summary email confirming shared diagnosis.

Required output: Causal problem map, role-play recording, and written post-call shared-diagnosis confirmation email.

Time estimate: 15 min pre-call prep · 30 min role-play · 15 min summary mapping (60 min total)

Evaluation rubric: Discovery preparation and hypothesis quality (20%); active listening and probing (30%); diagnostic accuracy of shared map (25%); ethical verification of fit (15%); post-call recap quality (10%).
