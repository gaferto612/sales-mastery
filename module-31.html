31Voice of Customer

Learn the market before you pitch it.

Turn interviews, lost deals, support conversations, and market evidence into a defensible view of customer progress, constraints, and alternatives.

45 min lesson + practiceEvidence-led professional curriculumLevel 3
In this module
  • 1. Build a research system
  • 2. Run non-leading interviews
  • 3. Map progress and forces of change
  • 4. Synthesize without inventing certainty
  • 5. Run credible win/loss reviews
  • 6. Artifact: an evidence repository

1. Build a research system

Separate four questions: who experiences the problem, in what circumstance, how they solve it now, and what evidence would justify change. Use interviews for depth, behavioral and CRM data for patterns, win/loss review for decision evidence, and surveys only after you know which questions deserve scale. Write a research brief: decision, participants, assumptions, interview guide, and stopping rule. Research is not a disguised sales call.

2. Run non-leading interviews

Ask for a recent concrete event rather than opinions about an imagined future. “Tell me about the last time…” produces a sequence you can inspect. Follow the trigger, search, alternatives, trade-offs, people, decision, implementation, and result. Avoid pitching or asking whether someone “would buy.”

Evidence ladder

Observed behavior and committed resources are stronger than remembered behavior; remembered behavior is stronger than stated intention; stated intention is stronger than a compliment.

3. Map progress and forces of change

Jobs-to-be-Done is a useful practitioner lens: buyers seek progress in a circumstance, with functional, social, and emotional dimensions. Treat it as a structured hypothesis, not a universal scientific law. Map the push of the current problem, pull of a new outcome, anxiety about change, and habit of the present solution. Verify each force with examples.

4. Synthesize without inventing certainty

Code notes by trigger, desired progress, current workaround, cost, risk, criterion, stakeholder, and exact language. Look for repeated mechanisms, preserve negative cases, and segment by meaningful differences in buying behavior. Tag every conclusion as observation, interpretation, or hypothesis; record sample limits and missing voices.

5. Run credible win/loss reviews

Interview customers and non-buyers soon after a decision. Ask how the shortlist formed, what changed, which criteria mattered, who influenced the choice, and what nearly reversed it. Do not reduce every loss to price or features; no decision, internal priority, implementation risk, and trust can dominate.

6. Artifact: an evidence repository

Create a research brief, five interview records, a coded evidence table, and a decision memo. State what changed, what remains uncertain, and which next experiment could disconfirm the conclusion.
