# Evidence Base — Quantified Findings, Effect Sizes, and Boundary Conditions

This file is the working evidence appendix for the curriculum. `RESEARCH.md` states the
*standard*; this file records the *findings* that modules rely on, with the number, the
source, and — critically — the boundary condition that stops the number from being
misused.

Every entry is classified:

| Class | Meaning | How the course may use it |
|-------|---------|---------------------------|
| **R** | Peer-reviewed research, meta-analysis, or systematic review | May be stated as a finding, with effect size and caveat |
| **L** | Law, regulator guidance, or official standard | May be stated as a requirement, with jurisdiction named |
| **V** | Vendor / practitioner dataset, not peer reviewed | May be cited as *observed in one dataset*, never as a law |
| **F** | Practitioner framework | Taught as a structure for thinking, never as proof |
| **X** | Claim that failed replication or lacks support | Taught explicitly as a thing not to believe |

## 1. What actually predicts selling performance

**[R] Verbeke, Dietz & Verwaal (2011), *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* 39(3), 407–428.**
Meta-analysis of the 1982–2008 literature, re-estimating the Walker/Churchill/Ford
classification. Five sub-categories held significant multivariate relationships with sales
performance:

| Driver | Standardized β |
|--------|---------------|
| Selling-related knowledge | .28 |
| Degree of adaptiveness | .27 |
| Role ambiguity | −.25 |
| Cognitive aptitude | .23 |
| Work engagement | .23 |

*Boundary condition:* relationships were moderated by measurement method (self-report vs.
objective), research context, and sale type. Note what is **not** at the top of the list:
extraversion, tenure, and generic "personality" measures. The practical reading is that
**what a seller knows and how they adapt outperforms who they are**, and that a manager who
removes role ambiguity is doing performance work, not administration.
Source: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-010-0211-8>

## 2. Buyer indecision, not competitor preference, is the main loss

**[V/R] Dixon & McKenna, *The JOLT Effect* (2022).** Analysis of ~2.5 million recorded sales
conversations. Of deals lost to "no decision," roughly **56%** involved a buyer who wanted
to change but could not carry the decision through — indecision rather than satisfaction
with the status quo. The mechanism the authors name is **omission bias**: people fear an
error of commission (choosing wrongly) more than an error of omission (failing to act).

*Boundary condition:* this is a proprietary practitioner dataset, not a peer-reviewed
sample, and the population skews to recorded B2B software conversations. The *mechanism*
(omission bias, ambiguity aversion) is independently supported in decision research; the
*percentage* should be treated as one dataset's estimate, not a universal constant.

**Course consequence:** the classic seller reflex — add more urgency, add more ROI — is the
wrong medicine for indecision, because it raises the perceived stakes of getting it wrong.
The evidence-consistent moves are reducing the option space, making a recommendation,
and de-risking the decision (see Module 09 and Module 10).

## 3. Influence principles replicate unevenly

**[R] Social proof / conformity** is the strongest of Cialdini's principles empirically.
Bond's (2005) meta-analysis of 125 Asch-type studies found a weighted mean effect around
*d* = 0.89; a 2023 replication by Franzen & Mader reproduced conformity rates close to
Asch's original ~33%.

**[R] Scarcity** replicates but modestly. A 2023 meta-analysis in *Psychology & Marketing*
(Ladeira et al.) found a mean product-scarcity effect of **r ≈ 0.28** (95% CI 0.22–0.32),
i.e. real but small-to-moderate, and highly moderated by product type and perceived
authenticity of the scarcity signal. Manufactured scarcity that buyers detect reverses the
effect via reactance.
Source: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mar.21816>

**Course consequence:** the course teaches influence principles as **compressed evidence
about a real situation**, not as levers. Social proof is legitimate when the comparable is
real, sourced, and genuinely comparable; scarcity is legitimate when the constraint exists
(capacity, cohort, implementation calendar). Both are misconduct when fabricated — see
Module 02 and Module 12.

## 4. Conversation structure: what recorded calls show

**[V] Gong / conversation-intelligence datasets.** Across large recorded-call corpora
(hundreds of thousands of B2B calls):

- Talk-to-listen ratio on won discovery calls clusters near **46/54** (seller talks less).
- Discovery calls that convert tend to contain roughly **11–14 questions**; both far fewer
  and substantially more (≈20+) associate with worse outcomes — interrogation is not
  discovery.
- **Speaker switches per minute** correlate with follow-up meetings more reliably than
  raw talk time: the signal is genuine dialogue, not seller silence.
- Winning calls tend to explore **3–4 customer problems**, not one and not ten.

*Boundary condition:* vendor datasets, observational, not randomized. Confounded by deal
quality (good-fit buyers talk more because they care). Treat these as **diagnostic ranges
for coaching**, never as targets to game. A seller who hits 46/54 by saying nothing useful
has learned nothing.
Source: <https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio>

## 5. The buying group and the seller's shrinking share of attention

**[V/F] Gartner B2B buying research.** Typical enterprise buying groups run **6–10 people**,
and can exceed 15 in large or regulated purchases. Buying groups spend roughly **17% of
total purchase time** meeting with any potential supplier — and when that time is split
across a shortlist, a single supplier may get around **5%**. Roughly **55% of buyers**
report encountering an overwhelming volume of trustworthy-but-conflicting information.

*Boundary condition:* Gartner survey research, self-reported time allocation, enterprise
technology skew.

**Course consequence:** the design implication is severe and is the backbone of Modules 06,
36, and 25. If you get 5% of the buying group's time, the artifact you leave behind — the
one they circulate when you are not in the room — is doing more of your selling than you
are. Sense-making (helping the group reconcile conflicting information) outperforms
volume of information.
Source: <https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/trends/challenger-make-sense-of-sense-making>

## 6. Negotiation: anchoring is real but smaller than folklore says

**[R] Orr & Guthrie (2005) meta-analysis** reported a correlation of about **r = .50**
between first offers and final outcomes — described by the authors as small-to-moderate by
Cohen's conventions once anchor-strength is accounted for. Individual lab studies show far
larger effects: Galinsky & Mussweiler (2001) found *r* = .85 between first offer and
settlement in a chemical-plant simulation.

*Boundary conditions that matter more than the headline:*

- The original meta-analysis included only ~19 effects and mixed anchors unrelated to
  first offers.
- Later work (2023–2025 syntheses) documents **costs** of aggressive first offers:
  worse subjective value, lower counterpart satisfaction, and higher impasse rates —
  which matter enormously when the "negotiation" is the start of a multi-year
  subscription relationship rather than a one-shot sale.
- Anchoring effects shrink as counterpart **information and expertise** increase. Against a
  professional procurement team with market data, the anchor is a starting position, not a
  spell.

**Course consequence:** Module 08 teaches preparation (BATNA, reservation value,
trade variables) as the dominant term and anchoring as a secondary, context-dependent one.
Sources: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749597825000603>,
<https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10726-023-09813-5>

## 7. Hiring: what predicts sales performance before you hire

**[R] Schmidt & Hunter (1998); Schmidt, Oh & Shaffer (2016) update.** The 2016 revision
lowered several magnitudes — notably work-sample validity — but preserved the ordinal
ranking. Highest multivariate validity combinations reported: GMA + integrity test (≈.65),
GMA + work sample (≈.63), GMA + structured interview (≈.63). Persistently near the bottom:
unstructured interviews, years of experience, years of education.

**Course consequence:** Module 41 builds hiring on structured work samples with behaviorally
anchored rating scales, and treats "great in the interview" as the weakest available signal.
Source: <https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/645/session%204/Schmidt%20&%20Oh%20validity%20and%20util%20100%20yrs%20of%20research%20Wk%20PPR%202016.pdf>

## 8. Retention economics: use the mechanism, distrust the slogan

**[X→R] "A 5% increase in retention increases profits 25–95%."** Traceable to Reichheld &
Sasser's work at Bain. The *direction* is sound and the mechanism is arithmetically obvious
(longer life × compounding expansion × lower re-acquisition cost). The *range* is not a
general law: it came from specific industry models with specific margin structures and has
never been established as a cross-industry constant.

**[X] Net Promoter Score as a superior predictor of growth.** Keiningham, Cooil, Andreassen
& Aksoy (2007, *Journal of Marketing*) failed to reproduce the claim that NPS outperforms
other satisfaction metrics; the 9–10 / 0–6 cut points are not statistically justified and
mask meaningful movement. Some later work finds NPS explains a moderate share of growth
variance in some industries — that is a much weaker claim than "the one number you need
to grow."

**Course consequence:** Module 11 teaches cohort retention, NRR/GRR, and time-to-first-value
as the primary instruments, and treats NPS as one weakly-calibrated signal among several.

## 9. SaaS operating benchmarks (2024–2025 medians)

**[V] Composite of KeyBanc, Benchmarkit, Bessemer, ChartMogul, OpenView reporting.**

| Metric | Median | Top quartile |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| CAC payback | ~16 months (improving from ~20–25 in 2022–24) | ≤ 6–12 months |
| SaaS magic number | ~1.37 (2025), up from ~0.90 (2024) | > 1.0 sustained |
| Blended CAC ratio | ~$1.30 S&M per $1 new ARR | < $1.00 |
| NRR (at scale) | > 100% is the pass mark, not the goal | 115–130% |

*Boundary condition:* survivor-biased vendor surveys, definitional drift (what counts as
S&M, what counts as new ARR), and heavy skew toward venture-funded North American SaaS.
Vertical SaaS recovers CAC slower than horizontal (≈18 vs. ≈14 months at the median).
Use these to sanity-check a model, never to set a target for a business with different
gross margins.
Source: <https://www.benchmarkit.ai/2025benchmarks>

## 10. Digital conversion: where the money actually leaks

**[V/R] Baymard Institute**, meta-analysis of 50 published cart-abandonment studies plus
16 years of large-scale checkout usability testing across 300+ top-grossing sites.

- Documented average cart abandonment: **~70%** (mobile ~80%, desktop ~66%).
- Leading stated reasons: **unexpected extra costs (~48%)**, forced account creation (~26%),
  payment security concerns (~25%), slow delivery (~23%), long/complex checkout (~22%).
- The average US checkout contains **~23 form elements** against an optimal **12–14**.
- Baymard estimates the average large site can gain **~35%** conversion from checkout
  redesign alone.

**Course consequence:** Module 03 teaches diagnosis of the *constrained* stage before
buying more traffic, because a 35% checkout gain applied to existing traffic is almost
always cheaper than a 35% traffic increase.
Source: <https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate>

## 11. Outreach is a legal activity before it is a creative one

**[L] United States — CAN-SPAM.** The FTC's business guidance is explicit that the Act makes
**no B2B exemption**. Requirements: accurate header/sender information, non-deceptive
subject lines, identification as an advertisement, a valid physical postal address, a
functioning opt-out honored within 10 business days. Maximum civil penalty per
non-compliant email was adjusted to **$53,088** effective January 2025.
Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business>

**[L] EU — GDPR + ePrivacy.** Requires a lawful basis. B2B outreach is commonly run under
*legitimate interests*, which is not a free pass: it requires a documented Legitimate
Interests Assessment (purpose, necessity, balancing test), transparency, and an
unconditional right to object. Maximum administrative fines: €20m or 4% of global annual
turnover.

**[L] UK — UK GDPR + PECR.** Corporate subscribers are treated more permissively than
individuals; sole traders and most partnerships are treated as individuals. Opt-out and
identification are still mandatory. See ICO direct-marketing guidance.

**[L] Canada — CASL.** The strictest of the four: express or implied consent is generally
required *before* the first commercial electronic message. Maximum penalties C$10m
(organizations) / C$1m (individuals).

**Course consequence:** Module 05 treats jurisdiction as a **field on the prospect record**,
not an afterthought, because a single EU or Canadian contact inside a US-designed sequence
collapses the compliance logic of the whole campaign.

## 12. Claims the course explicitly refuses to teach as true

**[X] Neuro-Linguistic Programming's core mechanisms.** Witkowski's (2010) review of 315
NLP-related publications found only ~18% supportive, with the majority contradicting NLP
claims. Sturt et al. (2012) systematically reviewed 33 health-outcome studies: 89% lacked
control groups and most had fewer than 20 participants. Specifically on **preferred
representational systems** — the "visual/auditory/kinaesthetic buyer" idea — Sharpley (1984)
reviewed 15 predicate-matching studies and found correlations typically below .30; Heap
concluded there "never has been any substance" to the conjecture that people have a
preferred internal modality inferable from word choice or eye movement.
Module 22 therefore teaches the *ordinary communication skills* (adapting pace, reusing the
buyer's own terms, offering the buyer a choice of evidence format) with the mechanism
claims removed.
Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3481516/>

**[X] Reading honesty, confidence, or objection from a single non-verbal cue.** Deception-
detection research consistently finds accuracy near chance for untrained and trained
observers alike. Module 04 teaches observation of *behavior sequences* and verbal
checking ("Did I get that right?") instead of cue-reading.

**[X] "Neuroscience" explanations for ordinary conversational effects.** Polyvagal Theory's
specific physiological claims have been challenged in the primary literature (Grossman,
2023). Module 28 keeps the practical instruction — reduce avoidable pressure, allow
silence, notice observable disengagement — and drops the biological mechanism.

**[X] Deterministic cultural or personality typing of buyers.** Module 42 uses cultural
frameworks strictly as *hypothesis generators* that must be verified with the specific
buyer in front of you.

## 13. How the course itself is designed (learning-science basis)

**[R] Roediger & Karpicke (2006), test-enhanced learning.** Retrieval practice produces
substantially better long-term retention than restudying the same material. This is why
every module ends in a scenario-based retrieval check rather than a summary.

**[R] Gollwitzer & Sheeran, implementation-intentions meta-analysis (d ≈ 0.65).** Specifying
*when, where, and how* an intention will be enacted materially raises follow-through. This
is why every module ends with a single concrete **Immediate action** rather than a list of
takeaways.

**[R] Work-sample and behaviorally-anchored assessment** (see §7) is why every module's
deliverable is an **artifact** — a written plan, a model, a script, a dossier — that can be
scored against a rubric, rather than a feeling of understanding.

## Maintenance

When a module states a number, that number belongs here with its class letter, its source,
and the condition under which it stops being true. If a finding cannot be given a boundary
condition, it is probably folklore and should be removed from the module rather than
softened.
