The Math of Outbound

Prospecting is a measurement system, not a universal activity quota. List quality, relevance, channel, offer, sender reputation, timing, and legal basis all affect results.

Track delivered messages, verified contacts, conversations, qualified meetings, opportunities, wins, opt-outs, and complaints separately. A higher meeting rate is not progress if fit and downstream conversion deteriorate.

Building the ICP List

Your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) is the foundation. Calling bad numbers or irrelevant titles is the #1 reason reps fail at outbound.

  • Firmographics: Revenue, employee headcount, industry, geography.
  • Technographics: What software do they already use? (e.g., "Companies using Salesforce but not Hubspot").
  • Trigger events: Recent funding, new executive hired, massive hiring push.

The Cold Call Framework

A cold call is an interruption. You have 5 seconds to earn 30 seconds, and 30 seconds to earn 5 minutes.

1. The Pattern Interrupt & Permission
"Hey Sarah, it's Alex from Acme Corp. I know I'm catching you entirely out of the blue. Do you have 30 seconds for me to tell you why I called, and then you can hang up on me if it's not relevant?" Why it works: Radical honesty lowers their defenses. They almost always say "Go ahead."
2. The Problem Statement (Not a Pitch)
"Usually when I speak to VP's of Sales like yourself, they are struggling with [Problem A] or [Problem B]. Are either of those on your radar right now?" Why it works: You aren't pitching a product, you are pitching a problem. If they have the problem, they will talk.
3. The Ask (Setting the Meeting)
"Based on what you just said, it sounds like we might be able to help. I don't want to take up more of your time right now. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat next Tuesday so I can show you how we solved this for [Competitor/Peer]?" Why it works: You respect their time, offer social proof, and make a low-friction ask.

Cold Email that Converts

Generic templates are dead. AI has flooded the inbox. To stand out, your email must be short, relevant, and extremely specific.

The 3x3 Rule

Spend 3 minutes researching the prospect to find 3 highly personalized bullet points before writing the email.

The winning structure:

  • Subject Line: 2-4 words. Lowercase. No tricks. (e.g., "your Q3 pipeline")
  • The Hook: First sentence is about THEM, not you. (e.g., "Saw your recent post about moving upmarket.")
  • The Value: One sentence on how you solve a specific problem they likely have.
  • The Ask: A soft, interest-based Call to Action. (e.g., "Open to learning more?" instead of "Can we meet at 2pm on Thursday?")

LinkedIn Social Selling

LinkedIn is not for pitching. It is for building familiarity and authority. If you pitch in a connection request, you will be ignored.

  • Optimize your profile: Your headline should be who you help and how, not just "Account Executive at X".
  • Engage before connecting: Like and comment thoughtfully on their last 2 posts.
  • The Connection Request: Leave it blank, or say: "Hey [Name], loved your recent post on [Topic]. Would love to connect and follow your content."
  • The Transition: Once they accept, wait a few days before sending a soft outbound message referencing a specific trigger.

The Omnichannel Sequence

It takes an average of 8 touches to get a meeting. A sequence coordinates your efforts across channels.

Day Action
Day 1 LinkedIn Connect + View Profile
Day 2 Cold Email #1 (Highly personalized) + Cold Call (Leave no voicemail)
Day 4 Cold Email #2 (Bump email: "Any thoughts on the below?")
Day 5 Cold Call (Leave specific Voicemail)
Day 8 Cold Email #3 (Value add: share a case study or resource)
Day 11 LinkedIn Voice Note or DM
Day 14 Cold Call
Day 17 Cold Email #4 (The Breakup: "Assuming this isn't a priority right now, I'll stop reaching out.")
