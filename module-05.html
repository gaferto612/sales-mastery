The B2B buying committee & Multithreading

In any deal over ~€10K, you're never selling to one person. Map the committee or your deal will die from a stakeholder you didn't know existed.

Multithreading is the practice of building relationships with multiple stakeholders simultaneously. Single-threaded deals (where you only talk to the Champion) are extremely fragile. If they leave the company or lose political capital, your deal dies. You must weave threads to the Economic Buyer and Technical Buyer early.

Champion
Internal advocate. Believes in you. Sells on your behalf when you're not in the room.
Economic Buyer
Controls the budget. VP, Director, CFO. Often only joins at the end.
End User
Will actually use the product. Opinion matters, but often doesn't decide.
Technical / Security
IT, security, legal. Can kill a deal silently.
Procurement
Final negotiator. Their job is to squeeze you on price.
The Blocker
Someone who doesn't want this to happen. Often invisible until they kill it.

The big B2B frameworks

SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham, 1988)

A questioning framework based on actual research into what top reps do. Still gold standard for discovery.

  • Situation — understand current state (sparingly; feels like a quiz)
  • Problem — uncover pains and dissatisfactions
  • Implication — explore what those problems cost them
  • Need-payoff — get the buyer to articulate the benefit of solving it

The genius: by the end, the buyer has explained to you why they need to buy.

MEDDPICC

The enterprise evolution of MEDDIC. A qualification framework — answers: "Should I keep spending time on this deal?"

  • Metrics — what numerically improves if we win?
  • Economic Buyer — have I met the money person?
  • Decision Criteria — what will they decide on?
  • Decision Process — what's the path to signed?
  • Paper Process — legal, procurement, and vendor onboarding path.
  • Identify Pain — what hurts now? How much?
  • Champion — who internally is fighting for us?
  • Competition — including internal builds and "do nothing".

Challenger Sale

Top reps don't just relationship-build — they challenge the buyer's worldview.

  1. Teach — bring a unique insight about their business
  2. Tailor — adapt the insight to their situation
  3. Take control — push back on price, timeline, process when needed

In complex sales, buyers don't know what they need. The best sellers reframe the problem.

Sandler Selling

Emphasizes not chasing every deal, getting "no" early, qualifying brutally.

  • Up-front contracts — agree on what each meeting will accomplish
  • Pain funnel — drill into emotional consequences
  • Reverse selling — when buyer pushes, you back off; this often pulls them in
  • Pain, Budget, Decision — qualifying triangle

Outbound prospecting in 2026

In B2B you often have to create demand, not just capture it.

Cold email

  • Personalization is non-negotiable (AI killed generic templates)
  • Subject line: short, specific, no tricks
  • Opening line: about them, not you
  • One clear, low-commitment ask (15-min call, not 60-min demo)
  • 5–8 touches over 2–3 weeks
  • Stop when they say no or unsubscribe

Cold calling

  • Best times: Tue–Thu, 8–10am or 4–5pm local
  • Opening: pattern interrupt + reason for call + permission
  • Don't pitch — set the meeting
  • Voicemails: short, specific, reason to call back

LinkedIn

  • Connect with personalization
  • Don't pitch on the connection request
  • Engage with their content first (comment, share)
  • Then ask for a short conversation

The discovery call structure

If discovery is great, the demo and close are easy. If discovery is weak, no amount of pitching saves the deal.

0–2 min

Set the agenda

"Here's what I'd like to cover: understand your situation, see if there's a fit, decide next steps." Up-front contract.

2–25 min

Discover (use SPIN)

Current process, what's working / not, what they've tried, cost of status quo, timeline, decision process.

25–30 min

Position lightly

Brief: how you typically help companies like theirs. Don't full-pitch.

30–33 min

Mutual qualification

Honestly assess fit out loud. If it's not a fit, say so. Buyers respect this enormously.

33–40 min

Next steps

Specific: who, what, when. Get it on the calendar before hanging up.

Target ratio: you talk 30%, they talk 70%.

PLG vs. SLG

Modern SaaS companies operate on two distinct funnels.

  • Product-Led Growth (PLG): Users sign up for a free trial or freemium tier without talking to sales. Sales teams (often called "Product-Led Sales") monitor product usage data and only reach out to users showing high engagement ("Product Qualified Leads").
  • Sales-Led Growth (SLG): Traditional enterprise motion. You prospect into cold accounts, run discovery, and demonstrate value before they ever touch the software.

The best modern reps can execute SLG for massive enterprise deals while simultaneously harvesting low-hanging PLG accounts.

Pipeline hygiene rules

  • Every deal must have a next step with a date
  • No movement in 14 days = at risk
  • Closed-lost is not failure — it's information. Always do a loss review.
  • Want 3–4× quota in pipeline for safety
  • Forecast accuracy beats optimism

Exercises

  1. MEDDIC a real deal. Use the scorecard above. Where are the gaps?
  2. SPIN drill. With a partner, sell a pretend product using only SPIN questions for 10 minutes.
  3. Stakeholder map. Pick a company you'd love to sell to. Map the likely committee with 5 real names from LinkedIn.
  4. Mock discovery call with a friend. Record. Track talk-to-listen ratio. Target 30/70.
