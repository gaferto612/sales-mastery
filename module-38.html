38Contracting

Turn verbal intent into an executable agreement.

Qualify formal evaluations, write decision-centered proposals, manage due diligence, and navigate legal terms without giving legal advice.

45 min lesson + practiceEvidence-led professional curriculumLevel 4
In this module

  • 4. Understand the contract map
  • 5. Manage redlines as trades

1. Write for the decision

Restate the agreed situation, outcomes, scope, evidence, responsibilities, implementation, commercials, assumptions, risks, and acceptance criteria. Tailor to the evaluation process. A long generic document transfers your internal organization chart to the buyer.

2. Qualify the RFP

Identify mandatory requirements, scoring, incumbent influence, access rules, timeline, budget, and business outcome. Decide bid, influence, partner, or decline. Respond exactly to the requested structure; make compliance easy to verify. Never misrepresent capability.

3. Prepare security and privacy evidence

Maintain architecture, data flow, access controls, encryption, incident response, continuity, subprocessors, retention, deletion, certifications, and exceptions. Route claims to qualified owners. Security questionnaires are risk conversations, not clerical obstacles.

4. Understand the contract map

Know the commercial purpose of scope, payment, term, renewal, termination, warranties, indemnities, liability, confidentiality, data protection, IP, audit, service levels, and governing law. Only authorized legal professionals should interpret or approve exposure.

5. Manage redlines as trades

Prioritize by risk and value. Keep one version, one issue log, owners, fallback language, and approval limits. Exchange concessions conditionally. Before signature confirm implementation resources, dependencies, success measures, invoicing, and kickoff.

6. Artifact: paper-process room

Create a bid/no-bid scorecard, proposal outline, security evidence index, contract issue log, approval matrix, and signature-to-kickoff checklist.
