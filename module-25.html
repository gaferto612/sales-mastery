25The Prescriptive Demo

Killing the harbor tour.

Feature-by-feature demos often make evaluation harder. A decision-focused demo starts with the buyer's job, shows only relevant workflows, makes assumptions visible, and agrees on the evidence needed for a next step.

The Harbor Tour Trap

A "Harbor Tour" demo is when a seller opens their software and says, "On the left, you'll see our navigation menu. Up top is the search bar. Over here is the settings tab..."

This is a disaster. The C-suite executive does not care about your navigation menu. They care that their churn rate is 15% and they are bleeding cash. Showing them irrelevant features induces cognitive overload and kills the deal.

The Tell-Show-Tell Framework

Elite Sales Engineers (SEs) and Account Executives use the Tell-Show-Tell methodology.

  1. Tell: State the business problem they told you about in discovery, and explain exactly what you are about to show them to solve it. "You mentioned your team spends 10 hours a week building reports. I'm going to show you how our AI builds them instantly."
  2. Show: Perform the fewest clicks possible to demonstrate the solution. Do not show them how the sausage is made; show them the steak.
  3. Tell: Summarize the value. "So, by clicking one button, your team gets those 10 hours back. How would that impact your quarter?"

The Last Mile (Contextualization)

A prescriptive demo must feel like it was built exclusively for the prospect. This is called the Last Mile of contextualization.

Instead of using dummy data named "Acme Corp," elite teams inject the prospect's actual logos, competitor names, and industry-specific jargon into the demo environment. When a VP of Logistics sees a demo populated with actual trucking routes instead of generic widgets, their brain instantly bridges the gap between software and reality.

Handling Derailments

Often, a mid-level manager will derail a high-level demo by asking highly technical, "in the weeds" questions. (e.g., "Can you show me how to change the background color of this specific field?")

The Parking Lot

Do not go down the rabbit hole. Acknowledge and defer. "Great question, John. Yes, we can fully customize the UI. Because we only have the CEO for another 15 minutes to cover the main ROI drivers, I'm going to put that in the 'parking lot.' Let's schedule a 15-minute technical deep-dive tomorrow so I can show you exactly how the UI engine works."

This protects the executive's time, maintains your status, and ensures the demo stays focused on massive business value.
