15AI Go-To-Market

Sell the workflow outcome, and price the accuracy honestly.

AI products fail commercially for reasons that have nothing to do with model quality: unstated accuracy, undefined failure handling, ungoverned data, and pricing that punishes the customer for succeeding. This module addresses each.

1. Why AI products lose winnable deals

AI products lose deals they should win for four recurring reasons, and none of them is model quality.

They are positioned as a capability rather than a workflow outcome. "We use large language models to understand your documents" describes the vendor's technology. It leaves the buyer to work out which of their problems this touches, how it fits their process, and what changes on Tuesday morning. Most will not do that work.

Accuracy is left unstated until the pilot exposes it. Every probabilistic system has an error rate. A vendor who does not state it is not hiding a small number; they are signalling either that they have not measured it or that they are hoping the buyer will not ask. Both readings are worse than the number itself.

Failure handling is undefined. The buyer's real question is rarely "how accurate is it?" It is "what happens on the 8% where it is wrong, who notices, and what does that cost me?" A product with 92% accuracy and a clean exception path beats one with 96% accuracy and silent failures, in every workflow where errors compound.

Data governance arrives too late. Security, legal, and privacy reviews are not a final hurdle in AI deals — they are frequently the deciding evaluation, and they are staffed by people who are rewarded for caution. A vendor who first thinks about training-data provenance when the questionnaire arrives has already lost time they cannot recover.

Notice that three of the four are honesty problems rather than engineering problems, which means the seller can fix them without a single roadmap change.

2. Observable learning objectives

By completing this module you will be able to demonstrate the following:

Position an AI product around a specific workflow decision rather than a model capability.

Conduct an accuracy conversation that states the error rate, the failure mode, and the exception path.

Design a pilot with pre-agreed success criteria, a baseline, and a defined failure condition.

Choose a pricing model that survives variable inference cost and does not penalise customer success.

Prepare the AI-specific governance evidence that enterprise and regulated buyers will require.

3. Positioning around a workflow, not a capability

The translation is mechanical once the pattern is visible. Take the capability, find the decision it changes, and describe the workflow before and after.

Table 1: Capability positioning versus workflow positioning

Capability framing (weak)Workflow framing (strong)What changed
"AI-powered document understanding.""Your claims handlers stop re-keying policy numbers from PDFs; the exceptions queue shows only the 6% the system is unsure about."Names the role, the removed task, and — crucially — what happens to the uncertain cases.
"Intelligent lead scoring.""Your SDRs work a list of 40 rather than 400, and you can see which 40 the model dropped and why."Names the changed daily behavior and makes the model's decisions inspectable.
"Generative AI for customer support.""Tier-1 agents get a drafted reply with the three source articles cited; they edit and send. Anything without a source article routes to a human without a draft."Defines the human's role and the abstention behavior, which is what support leaders actually ask about.
"Predictive maintenance using machine learning.""Your reliability engineer gets a ranked weekly list of assets to inspect, with the sensor evidence for each. Last quarter that surfaced two failures before they occurred and produced eleven unnecessary inspections."States the benefit and the false-positive cost, which is what makes the benefit believable.
The question that produces good AI positioning

"Who does what differently on Tuesday morning, and what happens when the system is wrong?" If you cannot answer both halves in one sentence each, you are selling a capability. The second half is the one most vendors omit, and it is the one experienced buyers weight most heavily — because they have bought AI before and been surprised by the exception load.

One further discipline separates credible AI positioning from the rest: name the tasks you are not doing. An AI product that claims to handle an entire function invites the buyer to imagine the hardest edge case in that function and assume you fail at it. A product that says "we handle the 70% of invoices that match a purchase order; the other 30% still need a human, and here is why" is far more believable and far easier to pilot.

4. The accuracy conversation buyers actually need

Most accuracy conversations go wrong because the vendor answers a metric question and the buyer asked a risk question. The buyer wants to know what it will cost them when the system is wrong.

Table 2: The five elements of a credible reliability position

ElementWhat to stateWhy buyers need it
1. The metric and its denominator"94% field-level extraction accuracy on invoices, measured against 2,400 human-labelled documents across 6 customers."An accuracy figure without a population and a measurement method is not a claim, and enterprise evaluators know it.
2. Where it degrades"It falls to about 78% on handwritten annotations and on scans below 200 dpi."Every model has a degradation profile. Stating it is what makes the headline number believable.
3. The failure mode"Errors are usually a mis-read digit, not a hallucinated invoice. It does not invent line items."The type of error determines the risk. A silent plausible error is far worse than an obvious one.
4. Abstention and exception path"Below a confidence threshold it declines rather than guesses, and routes to a queue. That is currently 6% of documents."A system that knows when it does not know is worth more than a marginally more accurate one that always answers.
5. Monitoring and drift"Accuracy is sampled monthly against a held-out set; you get the report. If it drops below 90% we tell you before you notice."Model performance changes as inputs change. A vendor with no drift monitoring is asking the customer to detect the failure.
The two claims that end credibility with a technical evaluator

"It's 99% accurate" without a denominator, and "it doesn't hallucinate." The first is unfalsifiable as stated; the second is a claim about a probabilistic system that no vendor can make honestly. Both are read by evaluators as evidence that the vendor either has not measured or is willing to overstate — and once that inference is made, every other number in your proposal is discounted.

The commercially useful reframe: the comparison is not against perfection, it is against the current process. Human invoice keying is not 100% accurate either, and most buyers have never measured their own error rate. Offering to measure it as part of the pilot is often the most valuable thing you can do — it establishes a real baseline, and it frequently reveals that the incumbent process is worse than anyone believed.

5. Pricing when your cost of goods is variable

AI products break the classic SaaS pricing assumption that marginal cost is near zero. Inference costs money per unit of usage, which creates a genuine tension: the pricing model that is easiest to sell may be the one that loses money on your best customers.

Table 3: Pricing models for AI products

ModelHow it worksStrengthFailure mode
Per seatFixed price per user.Familiar, predictable, easy to procure.Margin collapses if heavy users drive high inference cost. Also caps revenue exactly where value is highest.
Per unit of work (document, ticket, call)Priced to the thing the customer already counts.Scales with value; the customer already has the volume number, so the case builds itself.Requires a unit the customer trusts. Can create perverse incentives if it discourages usage that produces value.
Outcome-basedPriced per resolved ticket, per collected invoice, per booked meeting.Strongest alignment; often the easiest business case to approve.Attribution disputes are severe. Only workable where the outcome is unambiguously measurable and you control enough of the path.
Consumption / creditsPre-purchased usage drawn down.Protects margin directly; matches cost to revenue.Buyers dislike unpredictable bills, and finance functions dislike them more. Needs caps, alerts, and rollover to be procurable.
Hybrid platform + usageBase platform fee plus metered usage above an included allowance.The most common workable answer: predictable floor, protected margin, room to expand.More complex to explain; requires transparent overage rates or it becomes a drip-pricing dark pattern.

Two rules matter more than the model choice. Never price so that the customer is penalised for the behavior that creates value — if success means processing more documents, a punitive per-document rate teaches them to use you less. And publish the overage rate. An unstated per-unit cost that appears on the first invoice is drip pricing, which Module 12 covers as a regulated dark pattern rather than a pricing tactic.

6. Worked example: an AI proof plan that survived procurement

Composite Case Study

Scenario: A document-extraction vendor is selling into an insurer's claims operation. Two previous AI vendors have been piloted there and both failed. The buyer's evaluation team is sceptical and technically capable.

The Brayford thread · Cadence's roadmap · why the reporting gap was disclosed

The one number Cadence would not round

Cadence's matching engine is probabilistic: it ranks bank staff by predicted availability for a specific shift, drawn from historical acceptance patterns, stated preferences, and working-time constraints.

The stated accuracy was 81% of ranked suggestions accepted at the first or second call, measured across 14,000 shift gaps at six customers over ninety days. The degradation was volunteered in the same breath: accuracy falls to about 62% where a site has fewer than forty bank staff on its list, and Brayford's four Marlow homes were below that threshold.

The abstention behaviour mattered more to Sam Whitfield than the headline. Below a confidence threshold, Cadence declines to rank rather than guessing, and routes the gap straight to the agency workflow with the reason shown. At default settings that was 11% of gaps.

The pilot's failure condition was written before it started: below 70% acceptance at Ridge over six weeks, Cadence withdraws at no charge. Elaine cited that clause at the capital committee as the reason the proposal was fundable against a fire-safety programme.

7. Weak versus improved reliability conversation

Composite Case StudyScenario: A technical evaluator asks, "How accurate is it, and how do we know it won't hallucinate?"

Weak execution (unfalsifiable reassurance)

Seller: "Great question — accuracy is one of our biggest strengths. We're running at 99% plus, and because we use retrieval-augmented generation with our proprietary grounding layer, hallucination really isn't an issue with our architecture. Our enterprise customers have been running this in production for two years with no problems."

Why it fails: "99% plus" has no denominator, no task definition, and no measurement method, so it is not a claim an evaluator can test — which means they will assume it is the best number from the most favourable dataset. "Hallucination isn't an issue with our architecture" is a categorical claim about a probabilistic system that no vendor can honestly make; retrieval grounding reduces fabrication substantially, it does not eliminate it. "No problems in two years" is unfalsifiable and, to anyone who has run these systems, implausible. The evaluator's conclusion is not that the product is good; it is that the vendor either has not measured or will overstate — and every subsequent number gets discounted.

Improved execution (state the number, the degradation, the failure mode, and the abstention)

Seller: "I'll give you the number and the caveats, and then I'd rather you tested it than took my word.

On field-level extraction we measure 94.2% against 2,400 human-labelled documents across six customers. That's a mixed corpus — if I gave you the number for clean typed PDFs only it'd be 97.6%, and I don't think that's the honest figure for your mail intake.

Two places it degrades: handwritten annotations drop it to about 78%, and anything scanned below 200 dpi to about 71%. If a meaningful share of your intake is faxed, that matters and we should measure it before you commit to anything.

On fabrication — I won't tell you it can't happen, because it's a probabilistic system. What I can tell you is the failure shape: our errors are mis-read characters, not invented line items, and we test specifically for line-item fabrication in every release. I'll send you that test methodology.

The thing I'd actually focus on if I were you is abstention. Below the confidence threshold it declines rather than guesses and routes to a human queue with the uncertain fields highlighted — currently about 6% of documents at the default setting, and you can tune it. A system that knows when it doesn't know is worth more to your team than one or two points of headline accuracy.

And one question back: do you know your current human error rate? Most teams don't, and if we're going to measure this properly we need something to compare against."

Why it succeeds: Every number has a denominator and a method. The degradation profile is volunteered and specific enough to be tested — which the evaluator will do, and passing that test transfers credibility to everything else. The fabrication claim is honest about the class of system while being specific about the observed failure shape. Abstention is presented as the thing that actually matters operationally. And the closing question reframes the comparison from perfection to the incumbent process, while offering to establish a baseline nobody currently has.

Where this fails

Where even experienced sellers get this wrong

Experienced AI sellers state accuracy with a denominator and disclose degradation — and then quietly let the measurement population drift away from the deployment population.

Cadence's 81% was measured at six customers with mature bank pools. Brayford's Marlow sites had small, poorly-maintained lists. The number remained true and became irrelevant, and nothing in the way it was stated flagged the mismatch — because the caveat named a threshold (forty bank staff) rather than the buyer's actual position relative to it.

The honest form is not a caveat but an assignment: "here is the number, here is the condition that moves it, and here is where I estimate you sit on that condition." That last clause is the one sellers omit, because it requires them to predict a worse result for this specific buyer and say so before the pilot.

The test is whether your accuracy claim has ever caused you to lower a buyer's expectation in writing. If the degradation profile is disclosed but never applied, it is a disclaimer rather than a disclosure — legally safer and commercially useless.

The mechanism: Accuracy figures are properties of a measurement population, and buyers read them as properties of the product. A caveat that names a general boundary condition without locating the buyer relative to it satisfies the seller's honesty requirement while leaving the buyer's expectation unchanged — which is the outcome the caveat was supposed to prevent.

8. Practice: the AI product proof plan

Exercise goal: build a proof plan for an AI product that a sceptical technical evaluator and a cautious procurement function would both accept.

Applies to your real situation
  1. Workflow positioning. Write one sentence for "who does what differently on Tuesday morning" and one for "what happens when it is wrong."
  2. Name what you do not do. List the tasks in that workflow you explicitly leave to humans, and why.
  3. Accuracy statement. Metric, denominator, measurement method, and the source of the labelled data.
  4. Degradation profile. The two or three conditions under which performance falls materially, with numbers.
  5. Failure mode. The shape of your errors, and how you test for the dangerous class.
  6. Abstention design. Threshold behavior, exception routing, and what proportion abstains at the default setting.
  7. Baseline plan. How you will measure the customer's current process before changing it.
  8. Pilot design. Scope, duration, success criteria — and an explicit failure condition under which you withdraw.
  9. Governance pack. Training-data provenance, customer-data training commitment, inference region, sub-processors, retention, drift monitoring, model-change notification.
  10. Pricing. Model chosen, with the overage rate stated and a cap.

Completed exemplar (AI support-deflection product)

What we do not doNo auto-send. No billing or account-change actions. No tickets flagged as complaints or containing regulatory keywords. All stated up front.

Evaluation rubric: Workflow positioning with both halves answered (15%); explicit statement of what you do not do (10%); accuracy with denominator and method (20%); degradation profile with numbers (15%); failure mode and testing for the dangerous class (15%); abstention design (10%); pilot with a real withdrawal condition (10%); governance pack completeness (5%).

Beginner variation: Write only elements 1, 3, and 4. If you cannot state your accuracy with a denominator, that is the finding — take it to whoever can measure it, because you are currently selling a number nobody owns.

Advanced variation: Build the full governance pack as a standing artifact and measure how much it compresses your security-review cycle time across the next five enterprise deals. In most AI companies this is the single highest-return sales asset available, and it is usually nobody's job.

9. Applied knowledge check

1. Why is "99% accurate" a weak claim to a technical evaluator?

Because it has no denominator, no task definition, and no measurement method, so it cannot be tested — and an untestable number is assumed to be the most favourable one available. The credible form specifies the metric, the population, and how the ground truth was established: "94.2% field-level extraction against 2,400 human-labelled documents across six customers." Adding the degradation conditions makes it stronger still, because a number that comes with its own caveats reads as measured rather than marketed.

2. A buyer asks whether your system hallucinates. What is the honest answer?

Not "no." Any probabilistic generative system can produce a confident wrong output, and claiming otherwise ends your credibility with anyone who understands the technology. The honest answer has three parts: the shape of your observed errors (mis-read characters versus invented facts), how you specifically test for the dangerous class (a red-team set run every release, with the current rate), and what the system does when uncertain (abstain and route to a human, rather than guess). Buyers can work with a stated error rate; they cannot work with a vendor who denies the category.

3. Why is abstention often more valuable to a buyer than an extra point of accuracy?

Because it determines the operational cost of errors. A system that always answers distributes its errors invisibly through the workflow, where they compound and are discovered downstream by customers or auditors. A system that declines below a confidence threshold concentrates uncertainty into a visible queue that can be staffed, measured, and improved. This is why the practically important question is "what happens on the 6% you are unsure about?" rather than "is it 94% or 95%?" — the first determines headcount and risk, the second is a rounding difference.

4. What is wrong with pricing an AI product purely per seat?

Two things. Margin: inference has real marginal cost, so heavy users can be unprofitable while light users subsidise them — and your best customers are by definition the heavy ones. Revenue: per-seat pricing caps what you earn exactly where the value is highest, so success does not translate into expansion. The common workable answer is a hybrid — a platform fee for predictability plus metered usage above an included allowance — with the overage rate published and a cap, since an unstated per-unit cost appearing on the first invoice is drip pricing rather than a pricing model.

5. Why should an AI pilot include an explicit failure condition?

Because it is the most credible thing you can offer a buyer who has been burned before, and because it disciplines your own qualification. A pilot with only success criteria has no defined end — it drifts, gets extended, and consumes both sides' capacity while nobody wants to declare it failed. A stated withdrawal condition ("below 88% accuracy or handling time above baseline, we withdraw at no charge") converts an open-ended experiment into a bounded, reversible decision, which is precisely the move that addresses omission bias in a buyer who fears choosing wrongly.

10. Practical conclusion & next action

Selling AI well is mostly an exercise in being specific where the market is vague. The vendors that win the technically capable evaluations are not the ones with the best model; they are the ones who stated the number, named the degradation, disclosed the failure shape, designed the abstention, and offered to withdraw.

  • Position around the workflow: who does what differently on Tuesday, and what happens when it is wrong.
  • Name the tasks you do not do. Claiming a whole function invites the buyer to imagine your worst edge case.
  • Every accuracy figure needs a denominator, a method, and a degradation profile.
  • Never claim a probabilistic system cannot fabricate. State the error shape and how you test for the dangerous class.
  • Abstention beats a marginal accuracy gain in most real workflows.
  • Measure the customer's current process first. The comparison is against the incumbent, not against perfection.
  • Price so success is not penalised, publish the overage rate, and cap the exposure.
  • Have the governance pack ready in week one. It is often the deciding evaluation.

Immediate action: Write your accuracy statement with its denominator and measurement method, plus the two conditions under which performance degrades most. If you cannot complete that sentence today, you have found the highest-priority gap in your go-to-market.
