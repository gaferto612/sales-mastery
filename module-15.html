15Selling AI Products

Selling what AI built.

You've developed an app, a micro-SaaS, or a website using AI. Now you have to sell it. The playbook for selling AI-driven software differs fundamentally from traditional software because the technology itself is no longer the moat.

15 min read SaaS & Indie Modern Tech
In this module
  • The new moat
  • Positioning AI products
  • Pricing strategies
  • Go-To-Market (GTM)
  • AI-specific objections

We are in a golden era for makers. With AI coding assistants, single developers are shipping products that used to require teams of engineers. However, while the cost to build has approached zero, the cost to distribute and sell remains the same.

"When everyone has access to the same intelligence, the winner is the one with the best distribution and the deepest understanding of the customer's problem."

The new moat

Historically, software companies built moats with complex, proprietary code. In the AI era, relying on code complexity as a moat is dangerous. A competitor can build a clone of your app over a weekend.

If the code isn't the moat, what is?

  • Workflow Integration: Your app sits exactly where the user already works (e.g., inside their browser, their IDE, or their inbox).
  • Proprietary Data: You have data that the base AI models don't have.
  • Community & Brand: People trust you, follow you, and want to buy from you.
  • Speed to Market: You spotted a niche problem before larger companies could pivot to solve it.

Positioning AI products

A fatal mistake developers make is selling the mechanics of the AI instead of the outcome.

Rule of Thumb

Nobody wants to buy an "LLM-powered semantic analysis tool." They want to buy a tool that "writes your blog posts in 5 seconds."

When writing copy for your AI product, avoid leading with the underlying technology; position the product around user transformation.

Weak Positioning (Feature-focused) Strong Positioning (Outcome-focused)
"Powered by GPT-4 and vector databases." "Answers your customers' questions instantly."
"An AI wrapper for generating logos." "Get a professional logo for your startup in 2 minutes."
"Automated neural network image enhancer." "Make blurry photos crystal clear."

Pricing strategies

Pricing AI software is notoriously difficult because you have hard variable costs (API tokens, compute) that traditional SaaS doesn't have.

1. The Subscription Model (SaaS)
The classic monthly fee. This is great for predictable revenue, but dangerous if a power user burns through more API credits than they pay you in subscription fees. Solution: Cap usage or implement fair-use policies.

2. Usage-Based Pricing (Credits/Tokens)
The user buys 1,000 credits for $10. Every time they use your AI tool, it deducts a credit. This protects your margins perfectly but introduces friction, as the user constantly feels like they are spending money.

3. The One-Time Purchase (Lifetime Deal)
Often used to generate early cash flow to fund development. Extremely dangerous for AI tools unless the user brings their own API key (BYOK - Bring Your Own Key). If they use your API key, a lifetime deal could eventually bankrupt you.

Go-To-Market (GTM)

If you've built an AI micro-SaaS or tool, you need early adopters fast to validate the idea and fund the API costs.

  • Build in Public: Share your development journey on X (Twitter) or LinkedIn. Indie hackers love supporting other builders. Share your revenue, your bugs, and your wins.
  • Product Hunt: Launch your product on Product Hunt. A top-3 finish can drive thousands of highly qualified early adopters to your site in 24 hours.
  • Micro-influencers: Find creators on YouTube or TikTok who talk about "AI tools for [Niche]" and offer them free lifetime access in exchange for a mention.
  • SEO Programmatic: Since you have AI, use it to programmatically generate hundreds of landing pages targeting specific long-tail keywords (e.g., "AI logo generator for plumbers", "AI logo generator for bakers").

AI-specific objections

When selling AI to businesses (B2B), you will encounter a unique set of objections that don't exist in traditional software sales.

"Will this steal our data?"

The Reality: Enterprises are terrified of their proprietary data being used to train public models.
The Rebuttal: You must have a clear, documented privacy policy proving that you use enterprise APIs (like OpenAI's API, which explicitly does not train on API data by default) and that data is siloed.

"What if it hallucinates?"

The Reality: LLMs confidently lie.
The Rebuttal: Acknowledge it. Define exactly what the system automates, what evidence supports the claim, and which decisions require accountable human review.

"Why wouldn't we just use ChatGPT?"

The Reality: The biggest competitor to an AI wrapper is ChatGPT itself.
The Rebuttal: Sell the workflow, the UI, and the integration. "Yes, your team could spend 20 minutes writing perfect prompts in ChatGPT, or they could click one button in our app directly inside their email client."
