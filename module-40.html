40Channels

Sell with a system, not merely through it.

Choose the right partnership model, establish shared economics and rules, enable partners, and protect the end-customer experience.

45 min lesson + practiceEvidence-led professional curriculumLevel 5
In this module
  • 1. Choose the partnership job
  • 2. Assess partner fit
  • 3. Model two-sided economics
  • 4. Design rules of engagement
  • 5. Enable and measure
  • 6. Artifact: partner blueprint

1. Choose the partnership job

Distinguish referral, affiliate, reseller, distributor, agent, marketplace, implementation, technology alliance, and co-selling. Each solves a different access, capability, trust, or delivery problem. Do not use “partnership” to avoid a precise commitment.

2. Assess partner fit

Evaluate audience overlap, complementary capability, reputation, strategy, economics, capacity, compatibility, reach, conflicts, and customer value. A famous logo with no joint use case is not a channel.

3. Model two-sided economics

Document lead ownership, revenue share or discount, services, support, enablement cost, marketing funds, payment timing, renewals, expansion, and conflict. Both parties need a reason to prioritize the motion after launch.

4. Design rules of engagement

Define registration, attribution, territories, pricing authority, data sharing, brand use, conflict resolution, service levels, escalation, and exit. Make the end customer’s interests explicit; channel complexity must not trap them.

5. Enable and measure

Teach ICP, triggers, discovery, positioning, proof, qualification, demo, boundaries, and escalation. Certify demonstrated capability, not attendance. Measure activation, first-deal time, qualified pipeline, win rate, margin, implementation quality, and retention.

6. Artifact: partner blueprint

Create a partner thesis, fit rubric, joint value proposition, economics model, rules, enablement journey, first-90-day plan, and scorecard.
