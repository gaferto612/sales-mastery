The ego states.

The Three Ego States

According to Transactional Analysis (TA), during any communication, you are operating from one of three psychological states:

  • Parent: This state mimics the figures of authority from our past. It can be Critical (judging, demanding, telling people what to do) or Nurturing (protecting, comforting).
  • Child: This state mimics our childhood reactions. It can be Adapted (compliant, seeking approval, subservient) or Rebellious (defiant, acting out).
  • Adult: The objective, rational state. The Adult deals with the reality of the present moment without emotional baggage. It evaluates facts, asks logical questions, and treats the other person as an equal.

The Parent-Child Trap

The most devastating dynamic in B2B sales occurs when a buyer adopts the Critical Parent state: "Your pricing is ridiculous. You need to give me 30% off right now or we are done here."

Biologically and socially, when confronted by a Critical Parent, most salespeople reflexively snap into the Adapted Child state. They begin apologizing, seeking approval, and immediately offering concessions. "I'm so sorry, let me ask my manager, I really want to make this work for you."

The moment you enter the Child state, you have lost all leverage. You are no longer a trusted advisor; you are a subordinate begging for permission.

Adult-to-Adult (The Peer Dynamic)

Elite sales professionals exclusively operate in the Adult state, and their primary goal is to force the buyer into the Adult state with them.

The Adult Response

When the Critical Parent buyer demands a 30% discount, the Adult seller does not panic (Child) or get defensive (Critical Parent). They respond objectively: "A 30% discount is outside the scope of our standard agreements. However, if the budget is strictly capped there, we can review the implementation roadmap and remove the data migration module to match your target price. How would you like to proceed?"

By refusing the emotional bait, the seller forces the buyer to drop the Critical Parent act and engage logically (Adult-to-Adult) to solve the problem.

Crossed Transactions

When a buyer is acting like a Rebellious Child (ignoring emails, missing meetings, giving silent treatment), amateurs try to use the Critical Parent state to fix it ("You didn't show up to our meeting. We need to reschedule immediately.")

This is a "Crossed Transaction" and only deepens the conflict. Instead, use the Nurturing Parent or Adult state to disarm them: "Usually when communication drops off like this, it means priorities have shifted internally, or I missed the mark in our last demo. Which one is it here?" This gives them psychological permission to act like an Adult and tell the truth.
