The primal buyer.

B2B executives wear tailored suits and sit in glass boardrooms, but their decision-making engine is running on 300,000-year-old biological hardware. To truly master enterprise sales, you must sell to the primal drivers: survival, status, and tribalism.

Survival Hardware (Why We Fear Change)

For hundreds of thousands of years, the penalty for making a bad decision (eating the wrong berry, walking into the wrong cave) was death. Our brains evolved to be deeply risk-averse. This is the biological root of the "Status Quo Bias."

When you ask a VP to rip out their legacy software and replace it with yours, you are not just asking them to spend money. Their primal brain registers the change as an existential threat. If it goes wrong, they could be fired (exiled from the tribe), which historically meant death.

The Solution: You must make the Status Quo feel more dangerous than the change. If staying in the cave means starving to death (losing market share), they will venture out.

The Alpha Drive (Status)

Every member of a corporate hierarchy is unconsciously playing a status game. The drive to elevate one's status within the tribe is deeply wired into human biology because status dictates access to resources.

When you sell a massive digital transformation project, you are selling the buyer a mechanism to increase their status. You are giving them the "weapon" they need to conquer a new market, look heroic to their CEO, and secure their position as an Alpha in the organization.

Selling the Promotion

Always map your solution to the buyer's personal ambition. How does buying your product make them the hero of their own primal narrative?

Tribalism & Consensus

In modern enterprise sales, the average buying committee is 6.8 people. This is not just a corporate policy; it is biological tribalism. When the stakes are high, individuals seek the safety of the herd.

If the software implementation fails, but all 7 department heads signed off on it, no single person is exiled. The "tribe" made the decision. As a seller, you must actively foster this tribal consensus by multi-threading. You cannot just sell to the Alpha; you must align the entire tribe.

Bypassing the Neocortex

Pitch Anything author Oren Klaff popularized the concept that pitches are generated in your highly evolved Neocortex, but they are received by the buyer's primal Croc Brain (amygdala).

The Croc Brain's only filters are: Is this dangerous? Is it boring? Is it overly complex? If your pitch is a 40-slide deck of technical architecture, the buyer's Croc Brain flags it as boring and complex, and ignores it. You must deliver simple, high-contrast, emotionally resonant stories to pass the Croc Brain filter before the Neocortex will even process your ROI calculations.
